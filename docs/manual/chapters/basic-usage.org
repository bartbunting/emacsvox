* Basic Usage
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Basic Usage
:ALT_TITLE: Basic Usage
:DESCRIPTION: Learn the essentials in a speaking Emacsvox session.
:END:

Once Emacsvox is speaking, ordinary Emacs commands let you read, edit and
navigate. Emacsvox announces their results. Short speech commands inspect
text at your editing position, while tracked reading follows a longer passage.
This chapter moves from those reading choices to selecting and editing text,
then to everyday speech controls. [[*Work With The Audio Desktop][Audio Desktop]] explains how to keep several
tasks available through buffers and windows.

** Key Notation And Emacs Words
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE: no
:END:

Start with [[*Read the key instructions][Read the key instructions]] if the notation is unfamiliar. It explains
modifiers, uppercase letters and successive keypresses before asking you to
use them. [[*Answer a prompt and return to your work][Answer a prompt and return to your work]] covers command names,
completion and filename questions.

A /buffer/ holds text or an application view; /point/ is the position at which
movement and editing act. The selection guide below introduces the mark and
region when they are needed. For a separate introduction to Emacs editing,
~C-h t~ opens its interactive tutorial.

** Your First Few Minutes
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE: no
:END:

Try these in the speaking session started during installation:

1. Press ~C-n~ to move down one line and ~C-p~ to move back up. Emacsvox
   speaks the destination line.
1. Press ~C-e l~ to hear that line without moving.
1. Press ~C-e s~ to stop speech. Later commands still speak.
1. Press ~C-h k~, then a key, to learn what it does.
1. Press ~C-h m~ to discover the current modes and commands.
1. Press ~C-h C-e~ for the Emacsvox overview.

When you want to adjust speech, ~C-e H s~ opens Speech settings. Browse with
arrows and ask for an item's Help with ~?~ followed by its key. ~RET~ activates
it; ~q~ goes back and ~C-q~ closes the menu sequence. Browsing does not change
a value, but leaving does not reverse adjustments already applied. Detailed
scope and saving instructions follow below.

The keys in this manual are defaults. Use ~C-h k~ if one behaves differently.
The generated [[info:emacsvox-reference#Emacsvox Keymaps][Emacsvox Keymaps]] records default bindings; live Help reflects
installed packages and personal customisations. These menu quit keys apply
while an Emacsvox menu is active, not to every application buffer.

** Emacsvox Overview
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Overview
:ALT_TITLE: How Emacsvox Helps
:DESCRIPTION: Understand the main kinds of Emacsvox feedback.
:END:

Emacsvox speaks movement and command results. Integrations present information
in meaningful units such as messages, search results, tables, source code, and
Web pages. Voices can distinguish headings, links, comments, and other text;
auditory icons and tones can identify events without adding words. Pronunciation
dictionaries correct names and terms globally or for a mode or file.

Notice these distinctions before changing them. Compare structural voices in
[[*Hear Structure With Voice Lock][Hear Structure With Voice Lock]]. [[*Aural Presentation: Speech, Voices, And Sound][Aural Presentation]] explains speech,
sounds, rules, and reusable voice settings in detail.

** Using Emacs Buffers
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Working
:ALT_TITLE: Using Emacs Buffers
:DESCRIPTION: Understand feedback while editing and completing text.
:END:

Much editing feedback is automatic. Echo settings can speak each character or
each completed word. Completion announces candidates, inserted completion,
and the selected item. Prompts and error messages are also spoken.

Other information is requested explicitly:

- Reading commands speak a character, word, line, region or larger unit.
  Most inspections preserve point; tracked rest-of-buffer reading advances it.
  Names usually begin with =emacsvox-speak-= and default keys use
  ~C-e~.[fn::The prefix comes from Emacspeak, where C-e was mnemonic for
  the project name.]
- Speech-system commands adjust rate, punctuation, echo, and related controls.
  Many use ~C-e d~ and names beginning =tts-=.[fn::The d originally referred
  to DECtalk; this prefix now contains engine-independent controls.]
- Help commands explain the active modes, bindings, and integrations.

The settings menus provide discoverable routes to these controls; the direct
keys remain useful for frequent actions. ~C-e S~ opens focused settings for
the current integration, or general buffer settings when no focused menu exists.
~C-e H .~ always opens general buffer settings.

Buffers remain available as you change tasks. ~C-e n~ and ~C-e p~ cycle among
buffers with the same major mode; repeat with ~n~ or ~p~. See
[[*Buffers Hold Your Work][Audio Desktop]] for buffer selection and its other navigation controls.

** Reading And Navigating
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Reading
:ALT_TITLE: Reading And Navigating
:DESCRIPTION: Reading chunks of information.
:END:

Choose the amount of text you need to hear. Use a line or paragraph for a
local check, a selected region for exact editing boundaries, or ~C-e i~ for
longer reading with a tracked position. Ordinary movement speaks what you
reach; explicit inspection does not require that movement first.

The default keys are collected here. The following two sections explain
which preserve point, how to stop and start again, and the limits of large
requests. These distinctions matter before you begin selecting text.

*** Read a short passage without moving point
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Reading Without Moving Point
:DESCRIPTION: Inspect words, sentences and paragraphs while keeping your editing position.
:END:

#+cindex: reading without moving point
#+cindex: proofreading, spoken text
#+findex: emacsvox-speak-sentence

Use ~C-e l~ to check the line containing the next edit, ~C-e w~ to hear its
word, and ~C-e c~ to inspect its character. Repeating ~C-e w~ at the same
position spells the word. These are reading commands, not movement commands.
For a larger unit, ~C-e [~ reads the paragraph and
~M-x emacsvox-speak-sentence RET~ reads the sentence around point.

A positive prefix changes line, sentence or paragraph reading to start at
point. For example, ~C-u C-e l~ reads the rest of the line; ~C-u C-e [~ reads
the rest of the paragraph. ~C-u -1 C-e l~ reads from the line's beginning to
point. Enter the minus sign and digit as part of the prefix argument, not as
document text. The default form without a prefix reads the whole unit.

Emacs determines sentences, paragraphs and pages from the mode and text.
A page for ~C-e ]~ is an Emacs text page, not necessarily one screenful or a
printed page. That command also sets a page mark: do not use it while
preserving a selection for an edit. For more detail, see [[info:emacs#Sentences][Sentences]],
[[info:emacs#Paragraphs][Paragraphs]] and [[info:emacs#Pages][Pages]] in the GNU Emacs Manual.

For a bounded passage, set and inspect a region as described in
[[*Select and check the text first][Select and check the text first]], then use ~C-e r~. A short region read
preserves its endpoints and active selection. Speech follows pronunciation
and presentation settings, so silence is not proof that the selected source
contains no punctuation or whitespace. Inspect the boundaries before editing.

Whole-buffer reading with ~C-e b~, or the untracked rest-of-buffer form
~C-u C-e b~, is useful for short text. Neither supplies the interruption
position tracking described next. With the current default size limit,
buffer or region requests of 40,000 characters or more fall back to
windowful reading rather than reading the entire request. That helper removes
other windows and reads a screenful from point. Use smaller passages to retain
your window arrangement, or tracked reading when the backend supports it.
The limit is recorded in =ems--large-text-size=.

These commands read the buffer's accessible text. In Info, that is the current
node, not the entire manual; narrowing can also restrict a document. Folding,
pronunciation and mode-specific presentation affect what you hear. Use the
application's structural reader for tables, messages or other special views,
and [[info:emacs#Narrowing][Narrowing]] in the GNU Emacs Manual for restricted editing views.

*** Read from point, stop, and start again
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Reading From Point
:DESCRIPTION: Use tracked reading and understand where interruption leaves point.
:END:

#+cindex: continuous reading
#+cindex: read all from here
#+cindex: stopping and restarting reading
#+vindex: emacsvox-tracked-reading-max-chars

~C-e i~ starts reading at point and continues towards the accessible end of
the buffer. Unlike a line or paragraph inspection, it advances point as
sentence-oriented chunks complete. This requires a speech server supporting
tracked playback completion. An unsupported server reports that limitation;
it does not silently provide equivalent position tracking.

Try it in an ordinary prose buffer, with no selection you need to preserve:

1. Find the passage you want and finish any search or prompt. Use ~C-a~ for
   the beginning of its line and ~C-e l~ to check it. For the beginning of
   the accessible buffer, use ~M-<~ instead.
2. Press ~C-e i~ and listen without entering another command. The reading
   position advances by chunks, not by each spoken word.
3. Press ~C-e s~ to stop without moving to a different passage. Then use
   ~C-e l~ to inspect the retained line before editing or moving.
4. Press ~C-e i~ again to start from the retained position. Part of the
   last chunk can be repeated; this is not an exact-word pause/resume control.

Stopping retains the beginning of the current chunk, not necessarily the last
word you heard. Another user command interrupts tracked reading before
performing its own action. A movement command therefore also changes the
retained position, and ordinary typing can insert text there. Use the explicit Stop command when you
want to listen, stop, and decide what to do next. An existing active region
would change as point advances, so use ~C-e r~, not ~C-e i~, to check a selection.

Chunks prefer sentence boundaries and are bounded by
=emacsvox-tracked-reading-max-chars=, currently 280 source characters. This is
not a word count or an exact acoustic position. Completion reaches the saved
end of the accessible text; failure or interruption ends the reading session.
You can inspect the text and issue a new reading command without restarting
Emacs. On a backend without tracked support, read paragraphs or short regions,
or use the untracked buffer form with the size/layout limitations above.

For movement to another starting point, see [[info:emacs#Moving Point][Moving point]] in the
GNU Emacs Manual. The speech commands themselves belong to Emacsvox; their
exact interfaces are in [[info:emacsvox-reference#emacsvox-speak][the core speech reference]].

*** Hear Phonetic Names After A Pause
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Delayed Phonetic Reading
:ALT_TITLE: Hear phonetic names after a pause
:DESCRIPTION: Inspect characters with delayed phonetic names and cancel stale feedback.
:END:

#+findex: emacsvox-delayed-phonetic-mode
#+vindex: emacsvox-delayed-phonetic-delay

~M-x emacsvox-delayed-phonetic-mode~ toggles delayed phonetics. With it enabled,
character movement speaks the letter normally, then a pause gives its phonetic
name, such as "alpha" for "a". These are the English names also used by
~C-e c~.

The mode starts enabled with a one-second delay. Customize
=emacsvox-delayed-phonetic-delay= to another positive number of seconds and
save the mode preference through Customize. The menu at ~C-e H s t~ also
exposes delayed phonetics and its global delay, using a minimum 0.1 seconds
for numeric entry.

Further input, including the first key of a prefix, cancels the pending name.
New speech, stopping speech, edited source text, or leaving its buffer/window
also prevents stale feedback. Each pause produces at most one name. Typing,
deletion, punctuation, and explicit phonetic reading do not trigger the delay.

** Select, copy, and move text
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Editing Text With Speech
:DESCRIPTION: Check a selection, copy or move it, and recover a mistaken edit.
:END:

#+cindex: editing through speech
#+cindex: cut and paste

Reading a passage is not the same as selecting it for an operation. These
procedures make the affected text and destination explicit before changing
anything. They apply to ordinary editable text, not terminal input, a settings
editor or a table reader. Keep using the application's own instructions in
those contexts.

For practice, use ~C-x b~ to create an unused buffer named =evox-edit-source=,
run ~M-x text-mode RET~, and insert the following text, including the blank
lines and a final newline after the last sentence. Use another unused name
if that buffer already contains work. No file or speech-setting save is needed.

#+begin_example
Keep the opening paragraph.

Move this paragraph.

Keep the closing paragraph.
#+end_example

Keep the manual open separately and return to this buffer before trying a
step. [[*Answer a prompt and return to your work][Answer a prompt and return to your work]] explains command entry; the
[[info:emacs#Killing][Killing and moving text]] chapter in the GNU Emacs Manual supplies the wider
editing reference.

*** Select and check the text first
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Selecting Text With Speech
:DESCRIPTION: Set point and mark, hear the region, and check its newline boundary.
:END:

#+cindex: region, selecting and hearing
#+cindex: mark, checking a selection
#+cindex: newline, including in a selection
#+kindex: C-SPC
#+kindex: C-x C-x

Point is the moving end of a selection; the mark holds its other end. The
/region/ is the text between them, whichever comes first. This procedure uses
Emacs's normal Transient Mark mode, not the Transient settings-menu package.
Do not change either mode just because their names are similar.

1. In the practice buffer, press ~M-<~, then ~C-n~ twice and ~C-a~. Check
   with ~C-e l~ that you reached =Move this paragraph.=.
2. Press ~C-SPC~: hold Control and press the space bar. This sets the mark
   at the line's start. Release the keys, then press ~C-n~ once. Point is
   now at the start of the following blank line.
3. Press ~C-e r~ to hear the region. It contains the sentence and its
   terminating newline. Reading this short selection does not move its ends
   or deactivate it, so it remains ready for copying or cutting.
4. Press ~C-b~ once to move point back before that newline. The sentence
   is still selected, but its newline is not. ~C-e c~ can identify the
   character at this boundary; the character at the region's end is excluded.
   Press ~C-f~ to include the newline again.

If the operating system intercepts ~C-SPC~, ~M-x set-mark-command RET~ is
an alternative. Use a small selection and ordinary unshifted movement for this
exercise. Shift-selection has different deactivation rules.

~C-x C-x~ exchanges point and mark without changing the selected text.
Emacsvox speaks the line at the new point with point indication. You can
inspect that boundary, move it if necessary, and exchange again to return to
the other end. Starting a new selection with ~C-SPC~ replaces the boundary;
it is not the command for inspecting the old one.

In the ordinary text buffer, ~C-g~ deactivates the selection without deleting
text. It does not erase the saved mark. Some region commands can still use an
inactive mark, so always establish a fresh, checked selection before cutting.
Likewise, typing over selected text depends on Delete Selection mode; use the
explicit operations below rather than assuming typing will replace it.

More detail: [[info:emacs#Mark][The mark and the region]], [[info:emacs#Setting Mark][Setting the mark]],
[[info:emacs#Using Region][Operating on the region]] and [[info:emacs#Shift Selection][Shift selection]] in the GNU Emacs Manual.

*** Copy a passage into another buffer
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Copying Text Between Buffers
:DESCRIPTION: Preserve the source and check what is inserted at the destination.
:END:

#+cindex: copying text between documents
#+kindex: M-w
#+kindex: C-y

Emacs calls its remembered copied or cut text the /kill ring/. Copy with
~M-w~ and insert with ~C-y~, called /yank/ in Emacs. The names do not imply
that copying deletes anything.

1. Select the middle sentence and its newline using the previous procedure.
   Hear it with ~C-e r~, then press ~M-w~. Emacsvox reports the copy; the
   source text stays in place and the mark is normally deactivated.
2. Press ~C-x b~, choose an unused name such as =evox-edit-notes=, and run
   ~M-x text-mode RET~. Check the buffer with ~C-e m~. Use an empty buffer
   for this first copy, rather than inserting into an existing document.
3. Press ~C-y~. Emacsvox speaks the inserted text. Point is after it and
   the mark is at its beginning, normally inactive. Immediately press ~C-e r~
   to hear that range again; do not set a new mark first.
4. Return to =evox-edit-source= with ~C-x b~ and read the middle paragraph.
   It is still there. The destination contains an independent text copy.

For a real document, check the insertion point and deactivate any unrelated
selection before pasting. A copied newline moves subsequent text to another
line; a copied blank line can preserve paragraph separation. Inspect the
surrounding text as well as the pasted passage before saving.

During the transfer, ~C-e m~ and ~C-e l~ are useful checks. Avoid ~C-e f~
for filename inspection and ~C-e a~ for message repetition at this point:
those commands also put their result in the kill ring and can change the
next paste. ~M-x emacsvox-speak-current-kill RET~ inspects the current entry
without inserting it. Another application's clipboard copy can also change
what ~C-y~ inserts.

If the text goes to the wrong place, stop and use
[[*Recover a text edit before saving][Recover a text edit before saving]] rather than pasting more copies.
For the general commands, see [[info:emacs#Yanking][Yanking]] and [[info:emacs#Kill Ring][The kill ring]] in the GNU Emacs
Manual. [[*Keep a reference and a draft in separate windows][Keep a reference and a draft in separate windows]] offers another way to
manage the two working contexts.

*** Move a paragraph without losing it
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Moving Text Safely
:DESCRIPTION: Cut a checked passage, place it elsewhere, and verify both locations.
:END:

#+cindex: moving paragraphs
#+cindex: cutting text
#+kindex: C-w

Cutting with ~C-w~ removes the region and stores it for yanking. Practise in
the unchanged three-paragraph source above; copying in the previous task did
not alter it. Do not overwrite an existing buffer to recreate the example.

1. Select the middle sentence from its beginning. This time move down twice
   after setting the mark, stopping at the beginning of =Keep the closing
   paragraph.=. The region contains the middle paragraph and both following
   newlines, but none of the closing paragraph. Check with ~C-e r~.
2. Press ~C-w~. Emacsvox announces the cut. The source now contains the
   opening and closing paragraphs, and point is where the removed passage
   began. Inspect the remaining text without making another cut or copy.
3. Press ~M->~ for the buffer end. With the example's final newline present,
   press ~RET~ once to add a blank line after the closing paragraph. Press
   ~C-y~ to insert the cut paragraph there.
4. Read from the beginning using short passages. The order should be opening,
   closing, then the moved paragraph, separated by blank lines. Check that
   the middle paragraph appears only once.

In other text, decide deliberately whether to move a following newline or
blank line. Cutting only the visible words can leave an empty paragraph at
the source and join words at the destination. For a transfer between files,
verify both buffers before saving either: this is not one atomic file move.

Consecutive kills can combine into one kill-ring entry. Avoid using another
kill command to tidy the destination before yanking the passage; it can alter
what you insert. ~C-k~, for example, kills to the line end and can remove the
newline when used there. See [[info:emacs#Killing by Lines][Killing by lines]] and [[info:emacs#Appending Kills][Appending kills]] in the
GNU Emacs Manual before using that faster editing pattern.

*** Choose text copied earlier
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Choosing Earlier Copied Text
:DESCRIPTION: Retrieve a previous kill-ring entry without confusing insertion and replacement.
:END:

#+cindex: paste an earlier copy
#+cindex: kill ring, earlier entries
#+kindex: M-y

The kill ring keeps more than the latest passage. At the intended destination,
~M-y~ after a non-yank command opens a chooser for earlier entries. Use
completion or prompt history to inspect candidates, then ~RET~ to insert the
chosen text. ~C-g~ cancels before insertion. These entries may contain private
text; use a harmless example when practising or sharing your screen or audio.

Immediately after ~C-y~, the same ~M-y~ instead replaces that insertion with
an earlier entry. Further consecutive ~M-y~ commands cycle through earlier
entries, and Emacsvox speaks each inserted candidate. Once you issue a separate
reading or movement command, do not assume ~M-y~ will still replace the last
paste: outside the yank sequence it opens the chooser for another insertion.

To verify a candidate while cycling, listen to the automatic yank feedback.
When you decide to keep it, inspect its surroundings normally. If you have
already broken the sequence and want a different passage, undo the unwanted
insertion, check the destination, and choose again. Repeated ~C-y~ inserts
more copies; it is not an accept or preview command.

More detail: [[info:emacs#Earlier Kills][Yanking earlier kills]] in the GNU Emacs Manual. Kill-ring
history is useful recovery information, not a permanent backup of your work.

*** Recover a text edit before saving
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Recovering Text Edits
:DESCRIPTION: Undo deliberately in the right buffer and distinguish recovery from saving.
:END:

#+cindex: undo with speech
#+cindex: pasted into the wrong place
#+findex: undo-only

Undo belongs to the selected buffer. Finish prompts and leave special menus,
check the buffer with ~C-e m~, and press ~C-g~ in ordinary text to deactivate
any selection before undoing across the buffer. An active region can limit
undo to that region.

Use ~M-x undo-only RET~ to undo one recorded change, then inspect with
~C-e l~ or a freshly selected short region. Deactivate that inspection region
before the next undo. Repeat only as far as necessary. Insertion and deletion
can occupy separate undo records; a multi-step move can therefore need more
than one correction.

~C-/~ is the familiar undo key and works for an initial undo. However, an
intervening reading or movement command breaks its undo sequence, and a later
ordinary undo can redo what you just undid. =undo-only= avoids that reversal
when you pause to inspect through speech. ~M-x undo-redo RET~ deliberately
reapplies undone changes when the buffer's undo history permits it.

For an accidental transfer between buffers, undo the insertion at the
destination and the cut at the source separately, inspecting each. Copying
needs no source undo because it left the source unchanged. Neither operation
writes a file until you save. Correcting a saved edit changes the buffer again;
save the verified correction explicitly if the file on disk must change too.
Do not use Revert or close an unsaved buffer as a substitute for inspecting it.

The GNU Emacs Manual's [[info:emacs#Undo][Undo]] section explains history, selective undo and
limits. For file destinations and save failures, see
[[*Save a separate working copy][Save a separate working copy]]. Text undo does not reverse a settings-menu
save, a filesystem operation or an action already sent to a terminal.

*** Exchange text with another application
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Copying Through The Clipboard
:DESCRIPTION: Distinguish the Emacs kill ring from operating-system clipboard integration.
:END:

#+cindex: clipboard, exchanging text
#+cindex: WSL, clipboard boundaries

The kill ring is part of Emacs. The operating-system clipboard can connect it
to other applications when the display session and configuration support that
connection. Under normal graphical clipboard integration, ~M-w~ copies to both,
and ~C-y~ can insert text copied more recently outside Emacs. That is why
checking the pasted result matters even after checking the selection.

Test the boundary with a short, non-confidential sentence in each direction:
copy from Emacs and paste into a disposable external document, then copy a
different sentence externally and paste into an empty Emacs buffer. Verify the
actual text rather than assuming that success in one direction proves both.
Native Windows Emacs, WSLg, terminal Emacs and remote sessions need not share
identical clipboard behaviour. This guide does not install a bridge or change
your clipboard configuration.

If copying between Emacs buffers works but the external test fails, investigate
that display/clipboard boundary rather than changing speech settings. Do not
paste test commands into a live terminal. For the general integration and its
options, see [[info:emacs#Clipboard][Using the clipboard]] and [[info:emacs#Primary Selection][Cut and paste with other window applications]]
in the GNU Emacs Manual; a primary selection is not the same as the clipboard.

** Find and replace text carefully
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Searching And Replacing Text
:DESCRIPTION: Search deliberately and choose the scope before replacing occurrences.
:END:

#+cindex: searching, repeated matches
#+cindex: isearch, finishing before editing

~C-s~ searches forward and ~C-r~ searches backwards in the current buffer.
Type the query, listen to the match feedback, and repeat the search key to
reach another occurrence. Reverse direction with the other search key when
you overshoot. Searching the current text does not send a web or mail query.

When you find the occurrence you want, press ~RET~ to end the search before
typing document text. Forward search leaves point after the match; backward
search leaves it before the match. Check with ~C-e l~ before editing. With a
failing search, ~C-g~ first backs out of the failed part; another ~C-g~ may be
needed to abandon the search. Do not begin an edit while its prompt is active.

For details of repetition, search history, case and failure recovery, use
[[info:emacs#Incremental Search][Incremental search]] in the GNU Emacs Manual. Its rules describe the editor;
Emacsvox adds spoken match context and search cues. The next procedure changes
text, so it begins by establishing a narrower scope.

*** Review replacements one at a time
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Reviewing Text Replacements
:DESCRIPTION: Limit query replacement to a checked region and verify the changed text.
:END:

#+cindex: replacement, controlling scope
#+findex: query-replace
#+kindex: M-%

Use a disposable Text-mode buffer for this example. Insert these three lines:

#+begin_example
The draft is ready.
Keep this draft for comparison.
The draft outside the selection must stay unchanged.
#+end_example

1. From the beginning, set the mark with ~C-SPC~ and move down two lines.
   Point is at the third line's beginning. ~C-e r~ should read only the first
   two lines. Keep this region active for the next command.
2. Press ~M-%~ to start query replacement. At the first prompt enter =draft=
   and press ~RET~; at the second enter =version= and press ~RET~. These are
   answers to successive prompts, not text inserted into the document.
3. Emacsvox reads the line containing the proposed match. Press ~y~ to replace
   the first occurrence. At the second, press ~n~ to leave it unchanged.
4. When the command finishes, read all three lines. Only the first should say
   =version=. The second was skipped and the third was outside the selection.

~M-x query-replace RET~ is an alternative when Meta-percent is difficult to
enter. The active region limits the operation under normal Transient Mark
mode; without it, ordinary forward query replacement works from point towards
the accessible buffer end. Do not deactivate or replace the checked mark
between selection and starting the command. For a full-document operation,
first abandon unrelated selection and deliberately choose the starting point.

At a replacement question, ~q~ or ~RET~ stops without changing further
matches; earlier replacements remain. Comma, ~,~, replaces the current match
and lets you review it before advancing. ~!~ replaces the remaining matches
without more questions and is not needed for this guided task. Other editing
keys can leave the replacement loop and then act on the document. Rely on the
automatic match reading, or stop before issuing ordinary speech/editing keys.

Check the result before saving. Use [[*Recover a text edit before saving][Recover a text edit before saving]] if the
scope or replacement was wrong. [[info:emacs#Replace][Replacement commands]] and [[info:emacs#Query Replace][Query replace]] in the
GNU Emacs Manual cover exact matching, case behaviour and the full set of
answers. Regular-expression replacement is a different, more specialised task.

:END:

#+cindex: TTS
#+cindex: speech system

Use ~C-e H s~ for rate, punctuation, indentation, capitalisation, and typing
feedback. It starts with the captured buffer's settings; ~g~ switches scope,

retain their documented exceptions, such as global volume or phonetic delay.

Changing a global default is not necessarily saving it for restart. A menu's

still override defaults. [[info:emacsvox#Saving And Startup][Choose What Persists]] explains those distinctions.
The direct keys below are alternatives to menu navigation; use ~C-h k~ to
check a command's global prefix form before using it.

*** Adjust Rate And Spoken Detail

:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Speech Output Control
:ALT_TITLE: Adjust Rate And Spoken Detail
:DESCRIPTION: Adjust rate and how textual detail is presented.
:END:

Start with rate and punctuation, then adjust other feedback when the distinction
helps the task. Familiar prose may suit faster speech; exact code inspection
may need full punctuation. Direct commands affect the current buffer unless
their Help specifies a global prefix form.

#+attr_texinfo: :indic kbd :compact t

  Enter a rate with =tts-set-rate=. The menu alternative is /Rate/ in ~C-e H s~.
- C-e d DIGIT ::

  Choose one of ten predefined rates with ~0~ through ~9~.
- C-e d p ::

  Choose =some=, =all=, or =none= with =tts-set-punctuations=. A supporting
  host also offers named profiles, described below.
- C-e d s ::

  Toggle splitting of mixed-capital identifiers into words.
- C-e d c / C-e d C ::

  Toggle capitalisation indication with the lowercase key. The uppercase
  key chooses and previews spoken, tone, combined, personal-rule, or no
  indication. ~C-e H s c~ opens the Capitalization menu.
- C-e d i / C-e d I ::

  Toggle indentation feedback with the lowercase key, or open its menu with
  the uppercase key. ~C-e H s i~ reaches the same menu through settings.
- C-e d T ::

  Choose and preview /Alongside speech/ (default) or /Before speech/ for
  indentation tones. The current buffer changes; a prefix also updates the
  default. Save =emacsvox-indentation-tone-timing= through Customize for
  restart. Timing does not choose a spoken/tonal presentation or enable
  indentation. Backends without overlay support play tones before speech
  for either setting.

**** Use The Indentation Menu
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE: no
:END:

Open ~C-e H s i~ or ~C-e d I~. Select spoken columns with ~s~, pitch tones
with ~p~, or duration tones with ~d~; uppercase ~P~/~D~ combines speech and
tones. ~b~ places tones before speech and ~a~ alongside. Timing choices are
unavailable for presentations without built-in tones.

~g~ switches /Current buffer/ and /Global defaults/. Global defaults leave
existing local choices intact. A prefix starts the menu in global scope;
~e~ toggles indentation feedback in the displayed scope.

Up/down selects a control and left/right changes sections. Shift-left/right
on /Volume/ changes by five, bounded to 0–100. Other items give guidance rather
than changing volume. ~RET~ activates an item. These bindings apply inside
the menu, not in prompts or the source buffer.

~v~ or /Volume/ accepts an exact value; ~-~/~+~ remain shortcuts. Volume affects
all main-stream Omnivox tones even in buffer scope. Adjustments speak the
percentage and interrupt obsolete main speech, so repeated keys announce the
latest value rather than queueing old values.

Changes apply immediately. ~SPC~ previews four-column indentation even when
feedback is off. ~q~ leaves changes in effect. In global scope, ~S~ saves
indentation defaults and tone volume; closing without saving keeps defaults
changed only for this session. ~M-x emacsvox-set-indentation-presentation~
remains a direct alternative.

The following punctuation sections are reference material for changing individual
pronunciations. They are not prerequisites for ordinary reading. Reusable
palettes/profiles, engine-specific tuning, and the wider output model belong to
[[*Aural Presentation: Speech, Voices, And Sound][Aural Presentation]]. Specialist character-rate and chunking controls are
also available through live Help and the generated reference.

**** Edit Punctuation Pronunciations
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE: no
:END:

A punctuation level decides how much punctuation is spoken; the pronunciation
editor changes how particular characters are read. Open it with ~C-e H s p e~
or ~M-x omnivox-punctuation~.

This interface needs the bundled local launcher and the host's punctuation-editor
capability. Older hosts report that an update is required; remote and
native-direct configuration editing are not provided here. It displays saved
host settings and an unsaved draft separately. Running speech can still use
an earlier configuration.

Rows identify Unicode characters, pronunciations, and default/override origin.
The initial level is /some/.

#+attr_texinfo: :indic kbd :compact t
- l :: Choose none, some, or all, each with independent overrides; supporting hosts also offer named profiles.
- RET :: Speak a name, preserve the character, or restore its shipped default.
- a :: Add a character not already listed.
- d :: Restore the selected character's default in this level.
- s :: Save drafts on the host without restarting speech or altering unrelated configuration.
- r :: Restart this session's main and notification workers with saved settings after confirmation; save or discard drafts first.
- g :: Reread the saved file, asking before discarding edits.
- q :: Return while retaining the draft; reopening the editor resumes it. ~C-e H p d~ also resumes retained drafts.
- ? :: Read Help, including the selected host configuration path.

/Preserve character/ leaves pronunciation and pauses to the engine; it does
not promise silence. Org normally uses /some/. To name apostrophes, set both
/APOSTROPHE/ U+0027 and /RIGHT SINGLE QUOTATION MARK/ U+2019 to speak
“apostrophe” in /some/. That includes contractions. Omnivox 1.15.0 names straight,
curly, and modifier apostrophes at /all/ without a configuration file.

***** Save And Activate Changes
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE: no
:END:

Saving rejects a file or target changed since review and retains the draft for
reconciliation. Other configuration is preserved. A named-profile-capable host
writes Omnivox configuration version 4, unreadable by 1.16.0 and earlier. The
older built-in-only editor writes version 3, requiring 1.15.0 or newer. Do not
save a shared file with a format another selected executable cannot read.
Capability and configuration-format checks matter even when the main installation
uses a newer supported release.

The editor's ~s~ saves pronunciation data; its ~r~ activates through worker
restart. By contrast, ~S~ in the parent Punctuation menu saves the selected
level/profile through Customize, not the host editor's draft.

***** Work With Named Profiles
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE: no
:END:

With host support, ~N~ creates a profile inheriting =none=, =some=, or =all=.
Use a lowercase ID such as =prose= or =proofreading=. ~l~ switches levels and
profiles; ~X~ removes the selected profile from the draft. Profiles inherit
base-level edits and override individual characters. ~s~ saves all drafts
without changing speech; ~r~ loads the same definitions into both workers.

=tts-set-punctuations= offers profiles whose definitions agree in current workers.
Explicit selection applies to the buffer. A preference in
=tts-punctuation-mode-policy-alist= can use =(text-mode profile "prose" some)=,
retaining =some= as fallback for older hosts, missing profiles, or inconsistent
worker definitions. Status identifies fallback use. =tts-reset-punctuation-mode=
restores mode policy. Explicitly choosing a built-in level overrides a profile.
Character inspection is independent, and there is no built-in =most= level.

*** Stop Or Restart Speech
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Miscellaneous
:ALT_TITLE: Stop Or Restart Speech
:DESCRIPTION: Stop, silence, restart, or identify the speech server.
:END:

Stopping ends current output without disabling future feedback. Quiet mode
lets you work temporarily without speech. Restart after correcting a backend
problem or changing the selected synthesiser, not as an assumed cure for every
missing integration.

#+attr_texinfo: :indic kbd :compact t
- C-e s :: Stop current main and notification speech with =tts-stop=.
- C-e d q :: Toggle current-buffer quiet mode; ~C-u~ toggles it globally.
- C-e C-s :: Stop and restart the selected speech server.
- C-e d V :: Speak adapter and version information.

Server selection, repeated-pattern cleanup, text splitting, language selection,
and direct engine controls have specialist Help and reference entries. For
missing speech, follow [[info:emacsvox#Recovering From No Speech][Recovering From No Speech]].

** Hear Structure With Voice Lock
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Voice-lock
:ALT_TITLE: Hear Structure With Voice Lock
:DESCRIPTION: Hear document and program structure through voice changes.
:END:

Voice Lock starts automatically in supported buffers. It maps faces and
structural text properties to personalities, making comments, strings,
headings, links, and other text sound distinct without extra words.

Compare two styled elements in code, Org, Info, or EWW. Toggle ~C-e d v~ locally,
read the same elements, then toggle again to restore. ~C-e d C-v~ changes
Voice Lock globally. Disabling it removes that layer of voices, not ordinary
speech, semantic feedback, tones, or icons.

The result depends on engine and presentation settings. Voice Lock, explicit
face rules, and semantic rules are separate; see
[[*Voice Lock And Advanced Configuration][Voice Lock]] in Aural Presentation. Reusable voice, sound, and profile choices
belong to that chapter rather than to a Voice Lock toggle.
