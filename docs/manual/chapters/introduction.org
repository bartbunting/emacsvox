* Welcome to Emacsvox
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Introduction
:DESCRIPTION: Meet Emacsvox and choose where to begin.
:END:

#+cindex: Introduction

Emacsvox is a keyboard-driven spoken environment built on Emacs. You can use
it to read and write text, browse the Web, manage mail and messaging, work in
shells, program, and explore structured information through speech and sound.

Emacsvox requires Emacs 30.2 or newer and is derived from Emacspeak. Emacspeak's
pioneering audio-desktop design and T. V. Raman's original work remain the
foundation of this project.

Emacsvox works with Emacs commands and the structure of each application.
It speaks the text you reach and announces relevant changes, rather than
reading a screenful of characters indiscriminately. Distinctive voices mark
structure; short sounds can identify an event without adding a spoken label.

** Read the key instructions

:END:

Emacs manuals use a compact notation for keyboard instructions. Read this
section before trying the commands below. You do not type the notation as
text: it tells you which keys to press.

*** Hold modifiers; release them between steps
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE: no
:END:

A /modifier/ is a key held while you press another key. ~C-~ means Control,
usually labelled Ctrl. For ~C-n~, hold Control, press lowercase n, then
release both keys. The hyphen is part of the notation, not a key to press.

~M-~ means Meta, usually the Alt key. Some macOS configurations use Option
or Command for Meta. For ~M-x~, hold the key your Emacs uses for Meta,
press x, and release both. You can also press Escape, release it, then press
x; do not hold Escape. This alternative is useful when Alt is intercepted.

~S-~ means Shift. For ~S-TAB~, hold Shift while pressing Tab.
~C-M-f~ means hold Control and Meta together while pressing f. When several
modifiers are joined by hyphens, hold them for that one keypress.

*** Read spaces as separate steps
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE: no
:END:

Spaces between keys separate successive keypresses. They do not mean to
press the space bar. For example, ~C-x C-f~ means press Control-x, release
it, then press Control-f. Keeping Control held for those two letters also
works, because both steps require it.

The distinction matters in ~C-e H~:

1. Hold Control, press lowercase e, then release both keys.
2. Hold Shift, press h to type capital H, then release both keys.

Do not keep Control held for H. The capital H is a letter, not the name of
another modifier. Lowercase h and uppercase H are different instructions.
With the Emacsvox defaults, ~C-e h~ repeats Help, while ~C-e H~ opens
settings. ~C-h~ is different again: hold Control and press h; it begins
Emacs's Help commands.

A longer route such as ~C-e H v p~ starts the same way. After capital H,
press lowercase v, then lowercase p, with no modifier held. That route opens
Voice Palettes when used from an ordinary working buffer. Letters offered
inside a menu belong to that menu; typing p in a document normally inserts p.

A /prefix key/ starts an unfinished sequence. After pressing one, Emacs
waits for the next key. You may pause between steps; the notation does not
ask you to hold the whole sequence down at once.

*** Recognise named keys and command names
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE: no
:END:

~RET~ means Return or Enter, ~TAB~ means Tab, ~SPC~ means the space bar,
and ~ESC~ means Escape. Press the named key, not the letters of its name.
~<down>~ means the Down Arrow key; the angle brackets are not typed.
"Shift-left" means hold Shift and press Left Arrow. Where this manual says
"left/right", it offers either arrow, not a slash to type.

Some instructions run a command by name. In
~M-x describe-mode RET~, press Meta-x, release it, type =describe-mode=
with its hyphen, then press Enter. The name is ordinary text entered at the
command prompt, unlike the modifier notation. An instruction written
~M-x describe-mode~ also means to finish the name with Enter.

*** Cancel an unfinished instruction
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE: no
:END:

~C-g~ means hold Control and press g. It normally cancels an unfinished
key sequence or prompt. In Emacsvox's settings menus it goes back one menu;
~C-q~ closes the menu sequence. These are cancellation and navigation
commands, not undo: changes already applied remain in effect. An editor may
ask what to do with unsaved work, so read that prompt before answering.

~C-u~ before another command supplies a /prefix argument/. This is different
from an unfinished prefix key. Some commands repeat; others change their
scope or behaviour. Do not add it unless the instructions call for it.

Once speech works, ~C-h k~ followed by a key sequence explains its binding
in the current buffer without invoking that command. To check the menu
entry, enter ~C-h k C-e H~: Control-h, lowercase k, Control-e, then capital H.
Custom bindings and operating-system shortcuts can affect what reaches Emacs.
For more detail, see [[info:emacs#User Input][Kinds of user input]], [[info:emacs#Keys][Keys]], and
[[info:emacs#Arguments][Prefix arguments]] in the GNU Emacs Manual.

** Where To Begin
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE_NAME: Where To Begin
:ALT_TITLE: Where to begin
:DESCRIPTION: Get speech working and choose a learning or reference route.
:END:

Start with [[*Getting Started: Installation and First Speech][Installation]] if Emacsvox is not yet installed. It explains the
supported installation routes, takes you to a clean speaking session, and
provides recovery instructions when speech does not start. Once speech works,
[[info:emacsvox#Guided Tutorial][Learn the audio desktop]] teaches reading, editing, Help, structure, and
speech adjustments through six connected lessons and a disposable document.
Return to [[*Basic Usage][Basic Usage]], [[*Find Help While You Work][Using Online Help]], and [[*Work With The Audio Desktop][Audio Desktop]] as everyday references.

If Emacs itself is new to you, ~C-h t~ opens its interactive tutorial.
Emacsvox speaks the tutorial and your navigation while you follow it. At any
point, ~C-h m~ describes the active modes and ~C-h k~ followed by a key
explains its current command.

[[*Using this manual][Using this manual]] offers separate reading routes for new and experienced
Emacs users, with a short exercise in finding a topic and returning to work.
For one question, [[*Find a task][Find a task]] links everyday goals to their instructions.
Use [[*Terms used in this manual][Terms used in this manual]] when a name such as palette or profile is
unfamiliar. Detailed backend configuration comes later; it is not a prerequisite
for everyday use once speech works.

** The Welcome Screen
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE: no
:ALT_TITLE: The welcome screen
:END:

At an idle startup, after speech is ready, Emacsvox offers a short welcome
screen. Its actions test speech, adjust rate and output volumes, choose voices
and engines, open the tutorial or manual, and start normal use. Move between
actions with ~TAB~ and ~S-TAB~, activate one with ~RET~, and use ~q~ to
return. Arrow keys read the instructions normally.

Toggle /Show this welcome screen at startup/ to stop showing it automatically.
The choice is saved in this Emacs profile and survives application upgrades.
Open the screen again through Help's /Emacsvox welcome/ entry, ~C-h W~, or
~M-x emacsvox-welcome~. Startup leaves explicitly opened files and sessions
already in use alone.

** What You Will Hear
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE: no
:ALT_TITLE: What you will hear
:END:

Emacsvox combines several forms of feedback. Speech reads text, prompts,
results, and status. Voice changes make syntax and document structure audible.
Short auditory icons and tones identify events such as opening a file or
completing an action. Pauses and spatial cues help separate related information.

Begin with the supplied settings and adjust them as you learn what helps your
work. From the buffer you are using, ~C-e H~ opens the Emacsvox
settings menu. Press ~s~ for speech controls, ~v~ for voices and engines, or
~o~ for sounds and output. ~C-e S~ opens focused settings for the current
integration, falling back to general buffer settings when no focused menu is
available. To understand a particular item's feedback, leave point on it and
press ~C-e E~.

In a settings menu, up/down selects an item, left/right changes sections, and
~RET~ activates the selection. ~?~ followed by an item's key explains that
item; ~? ?~ explains the menu. ~q~ returns one level and ~C-q~ closes the
menu sequence. Leaving a menu does not reverse adjustments already applied.
Settings and editors have distinct saving rules, explained alongside each task
in [[*Aural Presentation: Speech, Voices, And Sound][Aural Presentation]].

Use ~C-e H a~ to find an action, or ~C-e H /~ to find a setting. Both select
the matching menu control before you activate it. Dedicated editors retain
their own navigation, preview, and saving commands; ~?~ explains each view.

Emacsvox sends speech through a speech server. New GNU/Linux, macOS, and WSL2
installations use Omnivox; established direct servers remain available for
compatible existing setups. Installation introduces what you need for first
speech. [[*Speech Backends][Speech Backends]] provides the platform and compatibility details.

** About This Manual
:PROPERTIES:
:TEXINFO_NODE: no
:ALT_TITLE: About this manual
:END:

The maintained chapters teach current setup and everyday tasks. Each explains
what you do, what feedback to expect, and how to inspect or recover the result.
The separate generated reference records commands, options, and keymaps from
the source. The heritage manual preserves Emacspeak essays and application
guides for their historical and design value; it is not a current setup guide.

Rapidly changing features are also documented in =README.org=, =etc/NEWS=,
and the generated Aural Presentation reference. When a copied binding differs
from your running Emacs, consult ~C-h k~, ~C-h m~, or ~C-h b~ for the
commands and bindings available in that session.
