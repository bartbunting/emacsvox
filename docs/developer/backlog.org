** TODO Share a complete presentation inspection model

*** Why this is needed

[[file:../../lisp/emacsvox-aural-explanation.el][Explanation]] now retains additional plans alongside its first-plan fields.
The =EVX-2026-021= through =EVX-2026-023= fixes added retained-part inspection,
captured-position guards and source-identity checks.  [[file:../../lisp/emacsvox-aural-change-feedback.el][Change Feedback]] already
reuses the input assembly and source-identity helpers in [[file:../../lisp/emacsvox-aural-tools.el][remapping tools]],
and the inspection service owns captured source access.  A complete common
view of parts, component eligibility, summaries and explanation basis is still
missing; future consumers must combine those pieces and choose the correct
source-validity policy.

*** Proposed plan

1. Define one internal inspection view over the complete retained plan list,
   selected part/component and explanation basis.  Adapt the existing
   first-plan accessors so callers can migrate incrementally.
2. Build on existing input assembly and identity helpers; consolidate the
   remaining component eligibility and part summaries for explanation,
   Recent Feedback, remapping and the guided workflow.  Preserve
   automatic selection when only one part is eligible and explicit selection
   when several parts qualify.
3. Move shared source-association policy to
   [[file:../../lisp/emacsvox-aural-inspection.el][the inspection service]], retaining distinct policies for current-item
   operations and historical feedback.  Current-item changes need their
   captured-position/edit guards; historical feedback may outlive the source
   and can associate with a surviving source by identity.
4. Keep live buffers and markers in temporary inspection state.  Preserve
   [[file:../../lisp/emacsvox-aural-history.el][history]] as bounded frozen data, without retaining dead source buffers.

*** Benefits

Every consumer would see the same parts, labels, cues and degradation.
Consistent selection and source association would reduce wrong-item edits and
make spoken inspection more predictable.  A new inspection command could reuse
these contracts instead of reconstructing them from several modules.

*** Disadvantages and decisions

A common model adds another representation and a migration period for old
accessors.  It must not erase the useful distinction between inspecting past
feedback and modifying the current item.  A single universal freshness check
could wrongly reject valid history or accept stale current-item edits.
Building summaries eagerly could also add cost for large retained records.

Preserve current prompts and summary verbosity during extraction.  Changes to
what users hear or which changes remain available after source edits need an
explicit interaction decision.  Overlay-only invalidation remains a separate
question requiring a contract and reproduction.

*** Acceptance checks

Reuse the native multi-part history regression in
[[file:../../test/emacsvox-aural-transport-tests.el][transport tests]] and the public remapping regressions in
[[file:../../test/emacsvox-aural-tools-tests.el][tools tests]].  Exercise labels, voices, cues and degradation found only in a
later part; multiple eligible parts; truncated history; moved point; renamed,
edited, killed and same-name replacement buffers.  Assert selected facts,
actual editor targets and spoken summaries.  Verify that historical records
retain no live source references and that current-item guards still reject
stale edits.  Run the focused inspection and guided-change suites as well.

** TODO Separate delivery preparation from process effects

*** Why this is needed

[[file:../../lisp/emacsvox-aural-transport.el][Transport]] combines span construction, suppression, effect transitions,
action positioning, wire encoding and dispatch preparation.  Its structured
and compatibility paths also interpret overlapping presentation policy.
=EVX-2026-020= showed that adding a silent run could change delivery for the
whole transaction and lose effects on surviving speech.  That bug is fixed,
but the interactions remain difficult to inspect in one large builder.

Commit =eb1e5cf3b= extracted the pure effect-state transition and explicit
delivery-field encoder, with source and compiled parity coverage.  The wrapper
still captures policy at its established point after resource resolution.
The complete builder, resource resolution, registration and process effects
remain coupled; the full delivery-candidate boundary is still future work.

*** Proposed plan

1. Preserve the extracted effect-state and delivery-field helpers.  Map the
   remaining builder inputs and effects, including run normalization and
   boundary-action placement, before proposing a complete candidate boundary.
   Capture current final-output behavior for any additional extraction and
   pass context/capabilities explicitly without moving their capture time.
2. Separate preparation of a complete delivery candidate from callback
   registration and process writes.  Model resource resolution explicitly as
   well: it may perform I/O, so it must not be hidden inside a helper described
   as pure.  Retain the existing all-or-nothing conversion and failure cleanup.
3. Make structured eligibility and compatibility fallback return an explicit
   internal result rather than requiring consumers to infer the chosen path
   from entries.  Preserve character positions internally and the deliberate
   conversion to UTF-8 byte offsets at the wire boundary.
4. Consider a later delivery-provenance extension recording requested style,
   compiled support, selected wire path and omitted dimensions for history
   and explanation.  Decide its owner, capture time, size bound and failure
   meaning in an ADR; implement that extension separately from extraction.

*** Benefits

Preparation could be exercised without sending speech or installing completion
callbacks.  Smaller steps would make suppression, coalescing and action
placement easier to reason about, including differences between structured
and compatibility output.  The optional provenance extension could help users
understand what a particular transaction actually submitted.

*** Disadvantages and decisions

This has the highest delivery-regression risk of the four proposals.  Moving
work across registration or send boundaries can change interruption timing,
duplicate callbacks or leave pending state after failure.  Intermediate data
structures can add allocations and copying on frequent navigation paths.
Measure representative long presentations before claiming a performance gain.

Keep lane ownership, stop/replacement policy and compatibility support intact.
The leading-whitespace/before-cue observation from remediation remains an
unconfirmed lead; establish its contract and reproduction separately so an
output change is not concealed as refactoring.  Delivery provenance must
distinguish submitted commands from confirmed completion and actual sound.

*** Acceptance checks

Compare captured final protocol output before and after each extraction using
[[file:../../test/emacsvox-aural-transport-tests.el][native submission tests]].  Combine styled text, silent/empty runs, boundary
and positioned actions, harmless run splitting, Unicode, punctuation cleanup,
coalescing and chunking.  Cover preparation/registration/send failure,
cancellation, late callbacks, replacement and independent notification output.
Normalize only opaque generated identifiers in comparisons, retaining action
ordering, positions and interruption semantics as assertions.  Require fresh
compiled Aural parity and all semantic golden traces to pass.  Automated
protocol comparisons do not replace human listening if behavior is changed.

** TODO Represent asynchronous voice applies as explicit operations

*** Why this is needed

=omnivox-apply-voice-configuration= in
[[file:../../lisp/omnivox-voices.el][the Omnivox adapter]] owns definitions, generation, pending processes, results,
timeout and completion through nested closures.  =EVX-2026-025= is fixed by
capturing the generation with its definitions.  An explicit operation would
make that ownership easier to inspect and make further lifecycle changes less
dependent on understanding every closure's captured variables.

Commit =d3f7c9383= also fixed timeout request cleanup and rejection of late
responses, with regressions for overlapping applies, replaced owners, send
failure and replies during sending.  The proposed operation record has not
been implemented; preserve these lifecycle fixes while extracting it.

*** Proposed plan

1. Introduce an internal operation record separating its immutable configuration
   snapshot from mutable progress: generation, per-process definitions, process
   identity, phase, pending responses, results, timeout and terminal state.
2. Extract named transitions for routing acknowledgement, registration,
   success/failure and timeout.  Each response must refer to its operation and
   original process; terminal completion must be guarded and published once.
3. Preserve the public callback/result shape, routing-before-registration
   ordering, partial-success reporting and retry behavior.  Migrate the apply
   path first; assess shared startup/preview machinery only after parity.

*** Benefits

Operation state would be directly inspectable in diagnostics and tests.
Generation ownership, per-lane progress and exactly-once completion would be
visible contracts.  Reordered replies and partial failure would be easier to
reason about without reading a long lexical scope.

*** Disadvantages and decisions

The record and transition helpers add code and explicit cleanup obligations;
closures currently provide useful encapsulation.  Retained operations could
hold callbacks, timers or dead processes longer than intended.  Over-generalizing
into a shared asynchronous framework would increase scope without a proven
benefit.

Preserve overlap and retry behavior during migration.  Serializing applies,
automatically cancelling earlier applies or suppressing their results would
change user-visible semantics and requires a separate decision.  Keep protocol
readiness distinct from acknowledged routing readiness under
[[file:../adr/0007-routing-ready-startup-checkpoint.org][ADR 0007]].

*** Acceptance checks

Preserve [[file:../../test/emacsvox-tts-tests.el][the existing apply regressions]] through the public API: FIFO and reverse
acknowledgements, both lanes, changed definitions, unchanged retries, partial
failure, timeout cleanup, late registration replies, overlapping applies,
replaced owners, second-phase send failure and replies received during sending.
Keep assertions on generation/content identity, per-process results, one
aggregate callback and retired timers/request state.  Add cases only for gaps
identified while mapping the operation, such as process death without a reply
or repeated terminal responses if not covered by the existing scenarios.
Check Workbench apply status and fresh compiled behavior.  Model-based receiver
checks prove the client contract; real backend lifecycle claims still need an
isolated connected check.
