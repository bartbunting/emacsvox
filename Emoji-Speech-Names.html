6.7 Name Occasional Emoji In Speech ¶

A voice such as Eloquence may need a fallback engine for an emoji in otherwise compatible text. Emoji naming changes the speech text before engine selection: ‘Forecast 🔮’ becomes ‘Forecast crystal ball’. The source buffer keeps its original text. This can keep the preferred voice speaking when the rest of the text is compatible; the normal repertoire checks still decide which engine can handle it.

The feature is enabled by default. Use M-x customize-group RET emacsvox-emoji RET to review its options. Set emacsvox-emoji-naming-enabled to nil to disable it globally, or disable it only in the current source buffer with: 

(setq-local emacsvox-emoji-naming-enabled nil)

To disable it for Org buffers through your configuration: 

(add-hook 'org-mode-hook
          (lambda () (setq-local emacsvox-emoji-naming-enabled nil)))

All four options support buffer-local settings. Set emacsvox-emoji-naming-enabled to nil locally to override an enabled global default. Voice previews opened from an aural screen use the remembered source buffer’s settings; contextual previews use their captured source policy. Changing a setting during playback does not change the submitted sample.

Enabled naming uses every exact name in Emacs’s emoji table, including ‘🌱’ (seedling), flags, keycaps and joined emoji. There is no need to approve each new emoji. emacsvox-emoji-approved-sequences defaults to t for this full table; optionally set it to a list of complete sequences to restrict naming. An unknown joined emoji stays intact, even when a component has a name. Arrow and harpoon symbols also receive names, including all directions, diagonals, double arrows and curved arrows. For example, ‘← ↑ → ↓’ becomes left arrow up arrow right arrow down arrow. Symbols outside the emoji table, such as ‘⇒’ and ‘⟶’, use their Unicode names. Text and emoji presentation selectors are supported; unknown joined or accented sequences remain intact. Box-drawing characters also receive their Unicode names: for example, ‘│’ becomes box drawings light vertical. This includes light, heavy, double, rounded and dashed borders and junctions. The same naming settings and limits apply; custom names can shorten names you use frequently. Foreign-language letters and unrelated symbols retain the existing fallback behaviour. Exact character inspection also retains its existing behaviour.

emacsvox-emoji-maximum-count defaults to 64 occurrences, counting repetitions separately. A subject such as ‘🤔Dinner? 🍕🍕🍕’ is spoken as thinking face Dinner? pizza pizza pizza, avoiding fallback caused by its four emoji. Above the configured limit, automatic naming is skipped for the whole item. One captured presentation object can contain several voice runs; a standalone preview sample is one item. Transport chunk sizes do not affect this limit. A sequence crossing a style or positioned cue boundary is conservatively left unchanged.

Names come from the emoji table and Unicode character data bundled with Emacs. Arrows prefer the emoji names when available; the four plain directional arrows share the names of their emoji equivalents. Coverage may vary between Emacs versions. Missing data or an absent exact name leaves the original sequence available to normal fallback; nothing is downloaded. The names are English, and enabled naming also applies to engines that could speak the original emoji.

Explicit pronunciation entries take precedence. For eligible sequences, set emacsvox-emoji-custom-names to override a bundled name, for example: 

(setq-local emacsvox-emoji-custom-names '(("🔮" . "prediction")))

Custom names must contain 1 to 80 characters and no control characters. Empty names are invalid; use an explicit pronunciation when you want to omit something. Explicit restriction and custom-name lists are limited to 64 entries, with sequences of up to 32 characters; the full-table setting has no entry limit. The occurrence limit accepts the range 0 through 64. Invalid settings skip automatic naming with an explanation rather than stopping speech.

In Feedback Details (C-e E), expand the relevant field to see the original sequence and its speech name, or why naming was skipped. The voice editor’s Details and last playback evidence also reports sample substitutions. These describe prepared text, not confirmation of what you heard. Play original keeps the retained speech text after settings change. Rule proposals retain that captured text while changing its presentation; open a fresh source presentation to try a different naming policy.
