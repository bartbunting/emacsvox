4.7 Lesson 6: preview and keep a voice experiment ¶

An installed physical voice is a voice an engine can produce. A palette’s named voice, such as default for ordinary reading, chooses physical voices and adjustments for a purpose. Browsing a physical voice does not replace that named entry. We will first try a physical voice without selecting it for ordinary reading or changing startup.

  1. From the practice buffer, press C-e H v b. Select an available engine and press v to browse its voices. Expand a language group with RET if needed, then choose an already usable voice.
  2. Press P to hear its sample and S to stop. An unavailable or inactive downloaded voice is not suitable for this first exercise. Read its details rather than enabling, installing, or applying packages as a workaround.
  3. Press t on the usable voice to open an experiment. If this resumes someone else’s or your earlier unfinished draft, leave it with Keep and choose another voice; do not overwrite or discard unrelated work.
  4. In your new editor draft, find Average Pitch and note its value. Use RET to enter 6, or 5 if it is already six. Press P to preview and B to compare the opening and edited versions with the same sample. S stops. An automatic sample may also follow an adjustment in the full editor; navigation stops it.
  5. Press u to undo that draft adjustment, compare again, then make the small adjustment once more. This is editor undo, not text undo. If the backend reports the adjustment unsupported, keep that limitation explicit and continue with browsing; do not infer that saving will fix support.
  6. Press q in the editor and choose to keep the unfinished draft. Return through the browser’s parent views with q until you reach its menu, then close the menu sequence with C-q. Ordinary reading is unchanged.
  7. From the practice buffer, press C-e H p d and select your retained experiment. Verify the value is still there. To finish without saving, leave its editor with q and choose to discard this practice draft only. Return through the remaining views and menus.

A retained draft lasts within the Emacs session. It is not a saved palette and does not survive restart. Discarding it leaves ordinary reading untouched, but discarding a draft would not undo a save that had already completed. The editor’s status distinguishes unsaved work, successful saving, and failed or pending application. Never treat a preview as proof of persistence.

4.7.1 Optional: save an inactive personal palette ¶

This exercise writes personal palette data. Skip it to keep the tutorial entirely free of saved speech-configuration changes. It deliberately avoids startup selection and does not require you to restart a working session.

  1. Open C-e H v p and note the active palette and whether selection follows the standard baseline or is explicit. Press N to create a palette with an unused name such as tutorial-reading, a short description, and acss-default as parent. Creation saves the new palette but does not activate it. Use another name if that one already exists.
  2. Enter your new palette with RET, select default, and press e to edit it. Change Average Pitch as above and compare with B. This entry controls ordinary reading, not every named voice in the palette.
  3. Choose Save to collection without selecting. Wait for its result and check the destination and saved status. Leave with q. The palette now holds your entry, but it is still not selected for ordinary reading.
  4. To try live selection deliberately, return to the palette manager with o, select your new palette, and press a. Return to the practice document and read the notebook line. Other named voices retain their own definitions. Rules or unsupported adjustments can make a contrast subtle; Inspect Or Adjust A Voice In Context explains how to investigate.
  5. Reopen the palette manager. If you originally followed the standard baseline, use f to follow it again. Otherwise select the original palette you recorded and press a. Do not use U, the separate startup action, during this tutorial. The inactive tutorial palette remains saved for later use.

If saving or application fails, keep the reported status and follow Recovering Unfinished Changes. Do not repeat Create, delete files, or restart Emacs to hide the failure. After an accepted save, text undo and draft discard are not rollback commands. Make a Personal Reading Palette gives the fuller lifecycle, including inheritance and startup selection.
