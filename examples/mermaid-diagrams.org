#+title: Exploring Mermaid diagrams with Emacsvox
#+startup: overview

* What this experiment does

The Emacsvox Mermaid experiment lets you explore a flowchart with speech.
You move between nodes, browse their connections, and ask how paths branch,
join or loop. The explorer reads the diagram's structure without requiring
an image or a browser. It describes explicit arrows and labels; it does not
infer what a process means or execute its steps.

This walkthrough starts with a single arrow and gradually adds more structure.
Each Mermaid block is a complete diagram you can explore directly in this Org
file. The examples are listening exercises: judge whether you can understand
the connections, keep your place and retrace your steps from speech alone.

The prototype currently supports Mermaid flowcharts only. It uses the pinned
Mermaid 13.0.0-alpha.3 parser, so this is experimental support. Other Mermaid
diagram types, such as sequence diagrams and Gantt charts, are outside its
current scope. Colors and visual styling are not narrated, and diagram links
and callbacks are not executed. See the [[file:../js/mermaid/README.org][prototype guide]] for full details.

* Open your first diagram

If this is your first time using the explorer, install its parser with Node
20 or newer and npm. Run this from the root of the Emacsvox checkout that
your Emacs uses.

#+begin_src sh
make mermaid-deps
#+end_src

In an up-to-date Emacsvox session you can now open a diagram. If you are
setting up a fresh checkout, run =make bytecode= from the same directory
and start Emacsvox from that checkout.

This file opens with its headings folded. Go to “1. Two nodes and one arrow”
and press Tab on the heading to expand it. Move to the line beginning
=flowchart LR=. Deactivate any selected region with =C-g=, then run
=M-x emacsvox-mermaid-explore=. The active region takes precedence over the
block at point, so a leftover selection can cause the wrong text to be parsed.
You do not need to execute the Org block or configure Org Babel rendering.

A separate read-only explorer opens and speaks an overview and the initial
node. Use the keys below in the explorer. Up and Down (or =p= and =n=)
browse the connections at your current node. Return follows your selection.
Press =q= to leave it, then move to the next block in this file and run the
command again. Each block is independent.

* 1. Two nodes and one arrow

An arrow points from its source to its destination. Here, Start has one
outgoing connection and Finish has one incoming connection.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart LR
    Start[Start] --> Finish[Finish]
#+end_src

At Start, press period (=.=) for full details. Press Right to follow its sole
outgoing connection to Finish. Press Right again. You should hear that there
is no outgoing connection and stay at Finish. Now press Left to follow the
incoming connection back to Start.

Press =b= at Start. It undoes your last visit and returns you to Finish,
with its previous selection restored.

Press =w= to hear where you are, =v= for the overview, or =?= for help.
The =LR= declaration specifies a visual layout from left to right. Explorer
navigation follows connections regardless of that layout.

* 2. A short sequence

A chain lets you practice following several steps and returning through them.
Terminal and database shapes also give speech a little more context.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart TD
    accTitle: Save a note
    accDescr: A note is written, stored and then confirmed.
    Begin([Begin]) --> Write[Write a note]
    Write --> Store[(Notebook)]
    Store --> Done([Note saved])
#+end_src

Follow Right through the chain. Listen for terminal and database shape cues;
ordinary box shapes are omitted from brief arrivals. Use period for full
node details. Press =b= several times to retrace your visits.

Use =f= and choose Begin, then press =e=. The explanation traces the straight
sequence ahead without moving you. Press =w= to confirm your position.
Press =v= and listen for the author-supplied description from =accDescr=.

* 3. A choice whose branches rejoin

This diagram has two choices at Known solution. Both paths lead to Resolve
the ticket. The diamond conventionally marks a decision; the arrows establish
the actual branch structure.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart TD
    Receive[Receive ticket] --> Known{Known solution?}
    Known -->|yes| Guide[Follow guide]
    Known -->|no| Investigate[Investigate problem]
    Guide --> Resolve[Resolve the ticket]
    Investigate --> Resolve
    Resolve --> Close([Close ticket])
#+end_src

Use =f= to find Known solution. Press =o= to summarize its outgoing choices
and select the first one. Up and Down browse choices without leaving the node;
Return follows the selected choice. Try the yes path, use =b= to return, and
try the no path. Press =e= at Known solution to hear where the branches rejoin.

At Resolve the ticket, press =i= and browse its two incoming connections.
Return follows the selected connection back to its source. Press =e= here
to hear the incoming paths described in forward order.

Press =C-g= to restore the combined connection list. That list puts outgoing
connections before incoming connections, followed by undirected links when
present. Listen for section announcements as you browse across a boundary.

* 4. A retry loop and a self link

A path can return to a node you have already visited. A self link returns
directly to the same node.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart TD
    Send[Send request] --> Reply{Response received?}
    Reply -->|yes| Done([Request complete])
    Reply -->|no| Wait[Wait before retry]
    Wait -->|still waiting| Wait
    Wait -->|retry| Send
    Wait -->|retry limit reached| Escalate[Escalate to an operator]
#+end_src

Find Response received and press =e=. Follow no to Wait before retry, then
use =o= and Up or Down to select still waiting. Return should keep you at
Wait before retry while traversing its self link. Press =o= again and choose
retry to return to Send request. Press =b= to return to Wait, then choose
retry limit reached to follow the exit to Escalate to an operator.

Listen for loop information with =e= at Wait before retry and =v= for the
whole diagram. Back should retrace the visits you actually made, even when
the same node appears more than once in that history.

* 5. Identical labels on different paths

Labels need not be unique. The identifiers R1 and R2 distinguish two Review
nodes, while edge labels give their paths meaningful names.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart TD
    Request[Receive change request] -->|manager| R1[Review]
    Request -->|security| R2[Review]
    R1 --> A1[Approved]
    R2 --> A2[Approved]
    A1 --> Schedule[Schedule work]
    A2 --> Schedule
#+end_src

Browse both paths from Receive change request. Listen for identifiers when
ordinary navigation needs to distinguish Review or Approved. Use =f= to
search for R2 and check that you reach the security review.

At Schedule work, press =e=. The explanation can use manager and security
to distinguish these paths without repeating every duplicate node's identifier.
Try =e= at Receive change request for the forward explanation as well.
The shared destination describes the graph; it does not establish that both
reviews must finish before scheduling can proceed.

* 6. Long paths and browsable explanations

Long chains can exceed the spoken explanation's tracing limit. This example
lets you expand one path while keeping the rest of the explanation short.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart LR
    Start[Prepare report] --> Collect[Collect figures]
    Collect --> Clean[Clean data]
    Clean --> Check[Check totals]
    Check --> Draft[Draft report]
    Draft --> Review[Review wording]
    Review --> Format[Format report]
    Format --> Send[Send report]
    Send --> Done([Report delivered])
#+end_src

At Prepare report, press =e=. With the default tracing limit of five arrows,
you should hear that later steps were omitted. Press capital =E= to open the
browsable explanation. Use Down to select the outgoing path, then Return or
=+= to expand it. Period repeats the selected row. Press =q= to return to the
explorer with its node and selection preserved.

For the reverse case, find Report delivered and press =e=. At the default
limit, earlier steps are omitted. Press capital =E=, move Down to the
incoming path and press Return to expand it. Press =q= to return. Expansion
affects only the selected path in that explanation buffer.

* 7. Different kinds of connection

Two arrows to the same destination are separate choices. A two-way arrow
allows travel in either direction. A line without arrows expresses an
undirected connection.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart LR
    Primary[Primary service] -->|normal| Worker[Worker]
    Primary -->|urgent| Worker
    Worker <-->|synchronize| Replica[Replica]
    Worker --- Notebook[Operator notebook]
    Worker -. status .-> Monitor[Monitor]
    Worker ==> Archive[(Archive)]
    Notebook o--o Maintenance[Maintenance port]
    Maintenance x--x Spare[Spare port]
    Primary ~~~ Anchor[Layout anchor]
#+end_src

At Primary service, use =o= and browse normal and urgent. Both lead to Worker,
but they should remain distinct choices. At Worker, browse the combined list
and listen for the two-way connection and the undirected link. Either Left
or Right can follow a selected two-way or undirected connection.

Browse the connections to Monitor and Archive to hear the dotted and thick
styles. Use =f= to find Maintenance port and browse both of its connections.
Listen for the circle marker on the link to Operator notebook and the cross
marker on the link to Spare port. The invisible =~~~= layout link is omitted
from navigation. Layout anchor remains available through =a= or =f=.

Use =f= to go back to Worker. Press =r= and choose Replica to preview an
arrow route. In the route buffer, Return follows the next step. Press =b=
to retrace it, then =q= to return to the explorer at Worker. Try a route
to Operator notebook. Its undirected link provides no arrow route there.
You can still visit it by following the selected undirected connection.

* 8. Separate parts and an isolated node

A single diagram can contain several independent components. Following arrows
from one component cannot reach the others.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart LR
    Browser[Web browser] --> Website[Website]
    Sensor[Temperature sensor] --> Display[Local display]
    Notebook[Operator notebook]
#+end_src

Press =v=. The overview should report three separate parts, including one
isolated node. Press =a= to browse all nodes, then select Temperature sensor
and press Return to visit the second component.

Press capital =F= and choose Disconnected nodes to find Operator notebook.
In this filter, disconnected means no visible connections or group membership;
it does not mean every node outside your current component. Try Entry points
and Exit points as well. The isolated notebook belongs to neither of those
filters.

* 9. Groups inside groups

Groups express containment. A connection to a group is distinct from a
connection to one of its members.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart LR
    subgraph Site[Warehouse site]
        Receive[Receive updates]
        subgraph Processing[Processing]
            Queue[(Work queue)] --> Worker[Process update]
        end
        Archive[(Audit log)]
    end
    Dashboard[Remote dashboard]
    Receive --> Queue
    Worker --> Archive
    Worker --> Dashboard
    Site --> Dashboard
    Processing -. status .-> Dashboard
#+end_src

Find Warehouse site, press =m=, and browse its members. Visit Processing,
then use =m= again to enter its members. From Process update, press =u= to
visit its containing group with the member you left selected. Use =w= to
hear the current node, groups and selection.

Find Process update and follow its outgoing connections. Listen for group
boundary announcements when moving to Audit log or Remote dashboard. At
Remote dashboard, compare the incoming connections from a node and from
groups. Containment helps describe the diagram's separate parts, but does
not create arrows for route finding.

* 10. Labels, Unicode and source positions

The adapter simplifies basic HTML and Markdown label styling for speech.
Unicode characters also exercise source navigation after characters that
occupy more than one UTF-16 unit in the parser.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart LR
    Start["Café reception 😀"] --> Read["Read<br/>the request"]
    Read --> Check["`Check **important** details`"]
    Check --> Save["Save &amp; notify"]
    Save --> Done["Finished"]
#+end_src

Follow the chain and listen for readable labels without basic formatting
markup. Pronunciation of the emoji depends on your speech setup. Find
Finished and press =s=. Point should land at that node's first source
occurrence in this block, despite the earlier emoji. Switch back to the
explorer buffer to continue. Unknown styling may remain literal.

* 11. A deployment workflow

This larger example combines branches, joins, longer paths, approval waits
and rollback. Labels describe a process, but navigation only follows arrows:
it does not run validation or evaluate approval conditions.

#+begin_src mermaid
flowchart TD
    accTitle: Deployment pipeline
    accDescr: Validation paths meet before staging. Approval can wait, and monitoring can trigger rollback.
    Commit([Submit change]) --> Build[Build application]
    Build --> Unit[Run unit tests]
    Build --> Lint[Check code style]
    Build --> Security[Check dependencies]
    Unit --> Gate{All checks passed?}
    Lint --> Gate
    Security --> Gate
    Gate -->|yes| Package[Package release]
    Gate -->|no| Fix[Correct the change]
    Fix --> Build
    Package --> Stage[Deploy to staging]
    Stage --> Smoke{Smoke tests passed?}
    Smoke -->|yes| Approval{Release approved?}
    Smoke -->|no| Fix
    Approval -->|yes| Production[Deploy to production]
    Approval -->|wait| Window[Wait for release window]
    Window --> Approval
    Production --> Monitor{Service healthy?}
    Monitor -->|yes| Done([Release complete])
    Monitor -->|no| Rollback[Restore previous release]
    Rollback --> Fix
#+end_src

Use =f= to visit each named node for these exercises.

1. At Build application, press =e=. Identify the three validation paths and
   their shared destination, All checks passed.
2. At All checks passed, press capital =E= and browse the yes and no paths.
   The yes path includes Package release and Deploy to staging, stopping at
   Smoke tests passed because that node branches. Press =q= to return.
3. At Release approved, press =e= and identify the wait loop. Some paths can
   keep looping instead of reaching a shared destination. Listen for the
   distinction between branches that rejoin and branches that can reach a
   destination while other paths avoid it.
4. At Service healthy, follow no through Restore previous release to Correct
   the change. Use =b= to retrace those visits.
5. At Correct the change, press =r= and choose Release complete. Browse the
   route's steps with Up and Down; this previews them without moving you.
   Return follows the next uncompleted step, regardless of the previewed row.
   Follow a few steps, retrace with =b=, then press =q=.
6. Use capital =F= to try Branch points, Joins, Upstream, Downstream and
   Unvisited. The first two describe arrow counts. Upstream and Downstream
   follow arrow paths relative to your current node.

Routes use the fewest arrows, with source order breaking ties. A route through
one validation branch does not establish that all checks have passed. Group
membership and undirected links do not supply route steps.

* Edit, refresh and listen again

The explorer holds a snapshot of its source. To try an edit, open the first
diagram and follow the arrow to Finish. Press =s= to visit its source.
Change =Finish[Finish]= to =Finish[Finished]=, keeping the identifier before
the bracket. Use =C-x b= to select the same explorer buffer (its name starts
with =*Mermaid:=). Press period to hear the old label. Try =s= again and
Emacs asks you to refresh first. Press =g= to refresh and hear Finished.

Refreshing preserves your current node when its identifier still exists,
clears traversal history and remembered selections, and invalidates explanation
and route buffers. Reopen those buffers after refreshing.

For an optional error exercise, temporarily remove the closing bracket from
=Finish[Finished]= and refresh. You should receive a syntax diagnostic, with
a line number relative to that diagram. Navigation becomes available again
after you restore the bracket in the Org buffer and press =g= in the explorer.

As you listen, note the example, current node, exact keys and what you expected
to hear. Check whether browsing leaves your position clear, branch labels give
enough context, Back restores the right selection, and longer explanations
remain useful. Automated parser and navigation checks cannot decide whether
the spoken experience is comfortable for you.
