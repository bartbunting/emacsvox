6.1 Getting Started With Aural Home ¶

For a first listening experiment:

  1. Press C-e H, then /, and choose Browse installed voices from the searchable actions.
  2. Select an available engine and press RET to list its voices.
  3. Move to a voice and press P to hear a sample. S stops it. Browsing and previewing save no settings.
  4. Press h to return to Home. From Home, C-c C-o returns to the item where you started.

If an engine is unavailable, its details explain why; try another available engine. If no speech is available, start with Recovering From No Speech.

Open Aural Home from an ordinary buffer with C-e H. Emacsvox remembers that source buffer and opening position so contextual actions such as explanation and remapping operate on the object you were using. Moving the source cursor while Home is open does not change this target. In Home, C-c C-o returns to it. To choose a new target, invoke C-e H afresh from that item in an ordinary buffer.

Returning through Home or Help preserves the selected row and unfinished edits. Buffer rule editors retain separate drafts for each source buffer; reopening a modified editor resumes its draft. Reopening the same voice in the voice editor likewise preserves its working settings. If a prepared remap’s source item changes or disappears, Emacsvox asks you to prepare that change again before applying it.

Aural Home begins with six task groups in one spoken table. Press RET or TAB to expand or collapse a group. Each action names a destination, reports its current status, and describes its purpose. / searches all actions, including collapsed groups; the matching group opens with your destination selected.

Expandable groups announce an open or close cue with their name when you reach or toggle them. These cues follow the icon setting and sound pack. For spoken state words, enable aural-panel-state-labels in Presentation Options. This adds “collapsed” or “expanded” after the control through the normal presentation rules. Key instructions remain available in Help.

Panel entry, return, selection, and warning cues accompany their spoken feedback in one utterance. Returning from a panel announces the destination and any unfinished draft warning together. These cues follow the icon setting, sound pack, and cue remapping rules. The voice editor uses the normal navigation voice for these announcements; auditions still play the working voice.

In Home and other tables, the navigation controls are:

n, p, down, up

Move by row, retaining and speaking the selected column. In the first column, settings lists announce the name and current state.

left, right

Move among the titled columns.

.

Speak the current column title and value.

SPC

Speak the complete current row.

RET

Open a destination, child list, or details.

C-c C-a

Choose an applicable action by name.

N

Create a new item where supported.

P

Preview in managers and editors; Home retains its profiles shortcut.

S

Stop the preview and its remaining samples.

?

Display and speak help for the current Aural interface.

h

Return to Aural Home from a manager or editor.

q

Go back or leave; an editor may first offer to retain or discard changes.

The six groups are:

Task groupDestinations
Understand and change feedbackExplain this item, Change this feedback, Recent aural feedback, contextual voice and sound changes, semantic vocabulary, and return to source.
Voices and speechVoice palettes, Browse installed voices, Preferred speech engine, Speech rate, and Voice Workbench.
Sounds and outputSound packs, Spatial settings, Notification output, Review notifications, and Stop notification speech.
Optional feedback and rulesPresentation options, Your feedback rules, Current buffer rules, and Explain feedback as you navigate.
Saved setups and unfinished changesPresentation profiles and Resume unfinished changes.
TroubleshootingAural Doctor, Spatial capabilities, and Omnivox engine modules.

The header identifies the captured source and the number of unfinished drafts. Choose Resume unfinished changes to visit the original rule editor, voice editor, or Workbench engine settings. The draft is still unsaved until you accept it there. Presentation Profiles capture options, palette, sound pack, and spatial settings. Physical choices and their adjustments belong to the selected palette’s named voices; shared engine settings are saved separately.

You can press RET on a row without memorizing its shortcut. Aural Home also provides direct keys for frequent routes, including x for explanation, O for your feedback rules, H for recent feedback, P for profiles, V for voice palettes, W for Voice Workbench, and D for Aural Doctor.

The voice editor contains fields and buttons rather than table columns: left/right changes a numeric value, and RET edits a field. See Edit a Named Voice. Keys are local to the view: for example, w saves a voice in its editor but exports a palette in the palette manager. Use C-c C-a to choose an action by name, or ? for that view’s current keys.
