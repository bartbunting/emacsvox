#+title: Semantic Aural Presentation Schemes
#+author: Emacsvox Contributors
#+date: <2026-07-25 Sat>

* Purpose

Build one semantic aural-presentation system for Emacsvox.

Speech labels, voice changes, auditory icons, pauses, ordering, and spatial
placement are alternative and composable ways to present meaning.  Modules
should describe what an object, state, or event means; a user-selected scheme
should decide how that meaning is rendered.

For example, an Org heading may be presented with any combination of:

- a distinct voice for its level;
- spoken text such as "Heading 1" before its contents;
- an auditory icon before or after its contents;
- a pause;
- a stable spatial position;
- a spoken or sounded indication that it is folded.

The system must not force those choices into mutually exclusive "voice",
"speech", or "sound" settings.  It must produce an ordered render plan that can
contain all of them.

This plan supersedes the narrower semantic auditory-icon theme plan.  Auditory
icon themes become resource providers within the broader system, and existing
ACSS personalities become one source of voice styling.  Both remain usable
through a staged, backward-compatible migration.

* Design Summary

The proposed flow is:

#+begin_example
semantic facts
      |
      v
matching scheme rules
      |
      v
backend-independent aural render plan
  +-- before: speech, cue, pause
  +-- content: speech plus voice and spatial style
  `-- after: speech, cue, pause
      |
      v
backend compilation and queueing
      |
      v
speech and audio services
#+end_example

The central design invariant is:

#+begin_quote
Resolve semantic facts, scheme rules, context, icons, and voice styles while
the source buffer and Emacs state are available.  Queue only a concrete,
ordered render plan.  The speech server must not need to understand modes,
modules, semantic names, schemes, inheritance, or user override precedence.
#+end_quote

* Goals

1. Define a stable semantic vocabulary for aural objects, events, states, and
   their attributes.
2. Let a module express meaning once without choosing whether the user hears
   speech, a voice change, a sound, spatial placement, or a combination.
3. Let schemes compose ordered actions before, during, and after spoken
   content.
4. Give users deterministic global, module, major-mode, derived-mode, and
   buffer-local customization.
5. Preserve ACSS as the device-independent voice description and preserve
   existing face-to-personality behavior during migration.
6. Turn auditory-icon themes into validated resource packs with documented
   semantic intent and fallback.
7. Resolve concrete voice commands and sound resources before queueing them.
8. Provide portable stereo positioning and optional richer spatial audio with
   documented fallback.
9. Make the system discoverable through completion, validation, preview,
   explanation, and an accessible scheme editor.
10. Keep existing one-argument icon calls, =personality= properties, face
    mappings, and bundled sound directories working while modules migrate.

* Non-Goals

- Replace the speech server or audio server in the first implementation.
- Rewrite every Emacsvox integration in one change.
- Move or rename the existing =sounds/= tree during the initial migration.
- Redesign the bundled audio samples.
- Make pronunciation, punctuation, capitalization, or language selection part
  of the first rule schema.  The render-plan contract should leave room for
  them, but existing TTS preprocessing remains authoritative initially.
- Require full three-dimensional audio on every backend.
- Require every scheme or sound pack to implement every third-party semantic
  extension.
- Infer module ownership from the Lisp call stack.
- Store arbitrary executable Lisp forms in portable scheme data.

Minor-mode selectors are deferred from the first implementation.  Several
minor modes may be active simultaneously, so they require an explicit ordering
and conflict policy rather than an accidental list order.

* Motivating Org Example

An Org module should be able to expose facts equivalent to:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(:role heading
 :module org
 :level 1
 :states (folded))
#+end_src

The exact public API and data representation are implementation decisions, but
the facts must not prescribe a presentation.

One scheme might add rules equivalent to:

#+begin_src emacs-lisp
(:id org-heading-level-1
 :match (:role heading :module org :level 1)
 :render
 (:before ((:kind speech :text "Heading 1")
           (:kind cue :name heading-1))
  :content (:voice voice-bolden
            :space (:balance -20))))

(:id org-folded-heading
 :match (:role heading :module org :state folded)
 :render
 (:after ((:kind speech :text "folded"))))
#+end_src

Another scheme could omit the spoken level, put the cue after the contents,
represent =folded= with a sound, or leave the heading unchanged.  The second
rule demonstrates composition: state presentation is independent of heading
level presentation.

This example establishes requirements rather than committing to these keyword
names:

- selectors can match roles, modules, attributes, and states;
- before and after actions are explicitly ordered;
- content can retain its text while receiving voice and spatial styling;
- independently matched rules can contribute to one render plan;
- a user can replace or suppress each contribution.

* Prior Art

** JAWS Speech and Sound Schemes

JAWS Speech and Sound Schemes associate controls, states, document elements,
and text attributes with voices, spoken text, or sounds.  Published examples
include piano notes for heading levels and a deeper voice for bold text.
Schemes can be selected by application.

The useful ideas for Emacsvox are:

- one user-facing scheme spans speech, voices, and sounds;
- rules are based on recognized semantics rather than file names;
- application-specific selection is visible to the user;
- preview is part of editing;
- training mode plays the configured presentation and then identifies the
  underlying semantic object or state.

The published JAWS editor exposes a fixed taxonomy and appears oriented toward
selecting a presentation for each recognized item.  Emacsvox needs an explicit
composition and ordering contract so that a heading can use speech, a voice,
and a cue together and so independent state rules can add presentation.

References:

- [[https://www.freedomscientific.com/SurfsUp/Speech_Sounds_Schemes.htm][JAWS Speech and Sound Schemes]]
- [[https://support.freedomscientific.com/support/technicalsupport/bulletin/513][JAWS scheme configuration by application]]
- [[https://blog.freedomscientific.com/increase-productivity-with-new-sound-features-in-jaws-and-fusion-2022/][JAWS split sound]]

** Aural CSS and CSS Speech

The CSS2 aural style model combines voice properties, =cue-before=,
=cue-after=, pauses, and spatial azimuth and elevation.  Its examples style
headings with both a voice and a preceding cue.  The CSS Speech module retains
voice styling, pauses, and cues and defines a portable stereo =voice-balance=
property.

Emacsvox already uses ACSS concepts for device-independent voice definitions.
The proposed system extends that architectural idea from voice alone to an
ordered aural presentation.

References:

- [[https://www.w3.org/TR/CSS2/aural.html][CSS2 Aural Style Sheets]]
- [[https://www.w3.org/TR/css-speech-1/][CSS Speech Module Level 1]]

* Current Architecture

** Voice and personality

=lisp/voice-setup.el= defines ACSS with the dimensions =family=,
=average-pitch=, =pitch-range=, =stress=, and =richness=.  =defvoice= creates a
named personality and customizable ACSS settings.  Engine adapters translate
the ACSS values into device-specific voice commands.

Text can carry a =personality= property.  If it does not, =tts-get-style= maps
its =face= property through =voice-setup-face-voice-table=.  Explicit
=personality= therefore takes precedence over face-derived voice styling.
=tts-audio-format= identifies style runs and queues the corresponding voice
commands and text.

This is already a useful separation:

- a face or module selects a semantic personality;
- ACSS describes it without depending on one TTS engine;
- an engine adapter generates concrete commands.

The missing pieces are a common semantic registry, contextual scheme rules,
ordered non-speech actions, spatial styling, and an explanation of why a voice
was selected.

** Auditory icons

=lisp/emacsvox-sounds.el= maps an icon symbol such as =open-object= to an Ogg
file in the selected directory.  =emacsvox-icon= either invokes a local player
or sends the resolved resource to the speech server.

The current cache is populated from =sounds/prompts/= and then the selected
theme.  Unknown names silently use =button=.  Theme selection knows the
hard-coded names =chimes= and =3d=.  There is no canonical intent registry,
scheme inheritance, per-semantic override, or common validation.

** Queued properties

=tts-audio-format= handles an =auditory-icon= property at the beginning of a
text run separately from =personality=.  It queues the icon and then formats
the text and voice.  This supplies only an implicit "before" ordering and
cannot express an after cue or spoken state annotation.

=tts-speak= copies text and its properties into = *tts-scratch-buffer* =.  It
captures the source =major-mode= in =orig-mode= for limited preprocessing but
does not make the scratch buffer use that mode.  Future mode-, module-, and
buffer-specific scheme resolution must therefore happen before the source
context is lost, or the required context must be captured explicitly with the
semantic annotation.

** Spatial audio

The =sounds/3d/= theme contains Csound-generated HRTF assets.  Several source
files under =sounds/3d/src/= use azimuth, elevation, panning, or movement.
=etc/pipewire/= also contains experiments with HRTF virtual devices and
left/right or upper/lower speech routing.

Spatial behavior is currently baked into assets or output devices.  It is not
a semantic property that a scheme can apply independently to a cue or speech.

* Terminology

** Semantic fact

A registered statement about meaning.  Examples include =:role heading=,
=:level 1=, =:state folded=, =:event open-object=, and =:severity warning=.

** Aural object

Content with an identity and lifecycle that may be rendered before, during,
and after its spoken text.  A heading is an aural object.

** Event

A semantic occurrence such as opening an object, completing a task, reaching a
boundary, or receiving mail.  An event may have associated content but need
not correspond to a persistent text range.

** State

A fact that qualifies an object or event, such as =folded=, =checked=,
=selected=, or =unavailable=.

** Scheme

A named, versioned collection of declarative rules plus references to resource
packs and voice palettes.  A scheme may inherit from another scheme.

** Rule

A selector and presentation contribution.  A selector matches semantic facts
and context.  Its contribution adds, changes, reorders, replaces, or suppresses
parts of a render plan.

** Render phase

One of =before=, =content=, and =after=.  Phases make order explicit without
requiring modules to coordinate separate speech and sound calls.

** Render action

A backend-independent instruction such as:

- speak literal text;
- speak the object's content;
- apply a voice or ACSS style;
- play a semantic cue;
- insert a pause;
- apply volume or spatial placement.

** Render plan

The ordered, fully merged result of semantic and scheme resolution.  It still
uses backend-independent voice and spatial values, but contains no unresolved
scheme rules.

** Concrete queue plan

The render plan after backend compilation.  Cue names have become file paths
or sample identifiers, voices have become supported engine settings or
commands, and spatial values have been reduced to backend capabilities.

** Sound resource pack

A named collection of concrete assets implementing registered cue names.
Existing =chimes= and =3d= directories become bundled resource packs.

** Voice palette

A named collection of device-independent voice styles.  Existing ACSS
personalities provide the initial palette.

* Semantic Contract

** Registry entries

The semantic registry is the authority for documentation, completion,
validation, and development-time auditing.  It should describe:

- a stable identifier;
- whether the entry is an object role, event, state, or attribute;
- concise user-facing intent;
- owning module for extensions;
- valid attributes and value types;
- valid states;
- lifecycle or allowed render phases where relevant;
- semantic fallback;
- optional default presentation;
- usage guidance and examples.

Semantic identifiers must not encode a chosen modality.  For example,
=open-object= can remain a useful event name, but a new identifier should not
be called =play-open-sound=.

Core names should express reusable interaction semantics.  A module registers
an extension only when no core meaning is suitable.  Extension registrations
must document an owner and a core fallback where one exists.

Registration collisions, invalid attributes, unknown states, and fallback
cycles must be diagnosed.  Development checks should reject new unregistered
literal semantics in the repository.

** Module contract

A module may:

- annotate a text range with registered facts;
- present a registered event with optional content;
- provide default rules scoped to its own semantics;
- register module-specific roles, states, attributes, or events.

A module must not:

- choose a sound filename;
- assume a bundled sound pack;
- infer that a semantic event will necessarily be a sound;
- depend on a particular TTS engine;
- hide undocumented meaning inside a newly invented cue name.

The emitting module supplies module context explicitly.  The resolver obtains
the source buffer and major mode directly while they are available.  Context
must not be guessed from the call stack.

* Scheme and Rule Contract

** Selectors

The first implementation must support selectors over:

- semantic role or event;
- state;
- registered attributes and values;
- module;
- exact major mode;
- derived major-mode ancestry;
- current buffer through a buffer-local rule layer.

Selectors may combine axes.  For example, a rule can target folded Org
headings without affecting headings rendered by another module.

Minor modes, window context, frame context, and workspace/project context may
be added after their ordering semantics are specified.

** Contributions and composition

Rules contribute to =before=, =content=, or =after=.  The merge contract must
distinguish scalar style properties from ordered action sequences.

Recommended initial behavior:

- scalar values such as voice, balance, volume, and speaking state use the
  most specific winning value;
- ordered actions compose in deterministic rule order;
- a rule can explicitly prepend, append, replace, remove, or suppress an
  action or phase;
- semantic identity is never changed by a presentation rule;
- silence can target a cue, speech annotation, content speech, a phase, or the
  complete presentation;
- duplicate suppression is based on stable action identifiers, not coincident
  text or file names.

The final data syntax and merge operators must be agreed and tested before
runtime integration.  Plain "last writer wins" is not sufficient because
independent rules such as heading level and folded state must compose.

** Cascade

Origin and selector specificity are separate concerns.  The recommended
origin layers, from strongest to weakest, are:

1. current-buffer and temporary session overrides;
2. persistent personal user rules;
3. active scheme rules;
4. module-provided defaults;
5. core fallbacks.

Within one layer, the resolver orders rules by documented selector
specificity:

1. combined module, exact mode, semantic identity, attributes, and states;
2. exact major mode;
3. nearest matching derived-mode ancestor;
4. module;
5. semantic identity, attributes, and states without context;
6. layer-wide defaults.

Additional selector fields increase specificity only according to a documented
comparison; registration order must not accidentally change behavior.  A
stable explicit rule order breaks genuine ties and is visible in the editor.

** Storage and versioning

Persistent scheme data must:

- carry a schema version;
- have stable scheme and rule identifiers;
- be readable without evaluating arbitrary code;
- preserve comments or user-facing descriptions where practical;
- support validation without selecting the scheme;
- support inheritance without mutating the parent;
- provide explicit migrations when the schema changes.

The native representation may be declarative Lisp data.  Import/export can use
another safe format later, but the semantic and merge contracts must not
depend on the serialization.

* Voice Integration

ACSS remains the device-independent voice layer.  The first implementation
should not replace =defvoice=, engine-specific voice adapters, or existing
=personality= text properties.

The renderer should accept:

- a named existing personality;
- a named voice-palette entry;
- an ACSS style or partial ACSS override;
- the default voice;
- =inaudible= where content suppression is intended.

Compatibility rules:

1. Text with no registered semantic annotation follows the current
   =personality=, then face-to-personality behavior.
2. Existing =personality= values become legacy presentation hints when the
   text also has semantic facts.
3. A user rule can deliberately override a legacy personality for matching
   semantics.
4. Face mappings remain a fallback source of presentation; visual face names
   do not become the canonical semantic vocabulary.
5. Backend compilation uses existing ACSS-to-engine adapters wherever
   possible.

The pure resolver should output a named or ACSS voice style.  The TTS adapter
then converts it to concrete engine commands before queueing.  This keeps
engine codes out of schemes while keeping the speech server simple.

* Cue and Resource Integration

The registry distinguishes a semantic cue from its concrete asset.  A resource
pack maps registered cue names to files and declares:

- name, version, summary, and optional parent;
- file or sample mapping;
- supported cues;
- required and optional coverage;
- format, channels, and duration where known;
- whether an asset is position-neutral, stereo, or already spatialized;
- fixed position or trajectory metadata for pre-spatialized assets;
- semantic fallback and aliases.

The default mapping may remain =CUE.ogg=.  Existing sound directories remain
valid resource sources during migration.

Theme selection must build a fresh effective resource cache.  It must not
retain an asset merely because the previous pack supplied it.  Unknown names,
unreadable files, aliases, missing required assets, and fallback cycles must be
diagnosed rather than silently becoming =button=.

A scheme may select a default resource pack and voice palette.  Users may
override either component without changing the scheme's semantic rules.

* Spatial Presentation

Spatial styling belongs to render actions and applies independently to speech
and cues.

The portable first level should be stereo balance with a documented range and
centre.  A richer optional level may add:

- azimuth;
- elevation;
- distance;
- width or spread;
- a time-varying trajectory.

The coordinate system must be listener-relative and documented.  Backend
capability fallback should be deterministic:

#+begin_example
3D position or trajectory -> stereo balance -> centred mono
#+end_example

Backends report capabilities.  The resolver does not inspect PipeWire, SoX,
Csound, or speech-server details; the backend compiler reduces the requested
style to what is available and records the degradation for explanation.

Pre-spatialized assets must not be spatialized a second time by default.
Resource metadata must distinguish position-neutral material from assets such
as the current HRTF-generated =3d= sounds.

Users need independent controls to:

- disable all spatial presentation;
- disable spatial speech while retaining spatial cues;
- reduce the maximum left/right separation;
- remap positions for unilateral hearing or speaker layouts;
- choose a non-spatial speech or cue fallback.

Stable positions are preferable to arbitrary motion because they can convey
source or role without reducing speech intelligibility.  Motion should be an
optional later capability, not a requirement for the first portable contract.

* Resolution and Queueing

Resolution has four stages:

1. Capture registered semantic facts and source context.
2. Match and merge scheme rules into a backend-independent render plan.
3. Resolve cues, voices, pauses, and spatial properties through selected
   resource and backend capabilities.
4. Queue a concrete ordered plan.

Concrete cue files or sample identifiers must be selected before queueing.
The speech server should only receive actions it can execute, such as a voice
command, text, silence duration, concrete sound resource, and supported spatial
parameters.

The queue contract must preserve the order of:

- before actions;
- content speech and its style runs;
- after actions.

If audio and speech use separate processes, the adapter must define whether
actions are strictly serialized, overlapped, or mixed.  A scheme must not rely
on accidental process scheduling.  Concurrent and background actions should
be explicit future action modes.

Immediate event presentation and text-property presentation must use the same
resolver and concrete-plan compiler.  A plain symbol-valued =auditory-icon=
property remains valid and maps through a compatibility adapter.

Context must be captured before =tts-speak= moves text into its scratch
buffer.  The implementation may resolve the complete plan at the call site or
attach an immutable context snapshot, but it must not attempt to recover the
source mode from the scratch buffer.

* Discovery, Explanation, and Editing

The user interface is part of the contract, not a follow-up convenience.

Provide commands equivalent to:

- =emacsvox-list-aural-semantics=: list roles, events, states, attributes,
  owners, and intent;
- =emacsvox-describe-aural-semantic=: describe registration, defaults, and
  available presentations;
- =emacsvox-explain-aural-presentation=: show captured facts, matching rules,
  cascade order, merge operations, concrete resources, and backend fallback;
- =emacsvox-preview-aural-rule=: render a rule against representative or
  current content;
- =emacsvox-list-aural-schemes=: show inheritance, resource packs, and
  validation state;
- =emacsvox-validate-aural-scheme=: report unknown semantics, invalid
  selectors, conflicts, unavailable voices, and missing assets;
- commands to add, edit, reorder, copy, enable, disable, and remove user rules
  at global, module, mode, combined, and buffer scopes.

The scheme editor should use an accessible Emacs buffer and completion rather
than requiring users to edit raw data.  Its workflow should be:

1. choose the semantic object, event, state, or current item;
2. narrow its context and attributes;
3. add and order before, content, and after actions;
4. select voice, speech, cue, pause, and spatial settings;
5. preview;
6. inspect conflicts and effective fallback;
7. save to the desired scope.

Customize may expose simple settings, but a dedicated editor is needed for
ordered actions, inheritance, rule explanations, and previews.

Add a training mode inspired by JAWS.  It should perform the normal
presentation and then speak a concise semantic explanation.  A more detailed
command should identify every matched rule and why it won or composed.

* Compatibility Policy

1. Existing =(emacsvox-icon ICON)= calls remain valid.
2. Existing symbol-valued =auditory-icon= text properties remain valid.
3. Existing =personality= properties and face-to-personality mappings remain
   valid.
4. Existing =defvoice= and ACSS settings remain supported.
5. Existing =chimes=, =3d=, and =prompts= directories remain valid resource
   sources.
6. Existing player selection remains supported.
7. Unknown legacy icons warn once and fall back during migration; new in-tree
   semantics must be registered.
8. Module context and richer semantics are optional until a module is
   deliberately migrated.
9. No repository-wide mechanical caller rewrite is part of an initial slice.
10. Compatibility adapters are removed only through a separately reviewed
    public API change.

* Test Gates

Every behavior-changing slice must run checks proportional to its scope:

1. Dedicated semantic, scheme, voice, resource, and render-plan ERT tests.
2. Full =make unit-test=.
3. =make trace-test= when speech or cue traces may change.
4. Complete Lisp byte compilation when public Lisp interfaces change.
5. Registry, scheme, resource-pack, and documentation audits.
6. Startup smoke tests after changing default scheme or provider selection.
7. Backend-specific integration tests when concrete queue plans change.

The dedicated tests must cover:

- unique registrations, ownership, valid fields, intent, and fallback;
- selector matching and derived-mode ancestry;
- origin-layer and specificity ordering;
- deterministic ties independent of hash or registration order;
- independent heading-level and folded-state composition;
- ordered before, content, and after actions;
- prepend, append, replace, remove, suppress, and silence;
- inheritance and cycle detection;
- buffer isolation and explicit module context;
- existing personality precedence and face fallback;
- named and generated ACSS voices;
- required sound coverage and optional extension fallback;
- unknown cues, semantics, voices, rules, and theme assets;
- clean cache rebuilding on provider changes;
- source-context capture before scratch-buffer processing;
- concrete resolution before queueing;
- queued and immediate equivalence;
- SoX, Pulse/PipeWire, and speech-server adapters;
- stereo, mono, unsupported spatial capability, and pre-spatialized assets;
- compatibility of existing icon calls, text properties, voices, and sound
  directories;
- explanation provenance sufficient to reproduce every winning decision.

* Open Decisions

These decisions must be resolved before their corresponding implementation
slice:

1. The final declarative rule, render-plan, and semantic text-property shapes.
2. The exact specificity comparison for selectors with different attributes
   and states.
3. Default composition behavior for multiple sequence contributions and the
   identifiers used to replace or remove an inherited action.
4. Whether module defaults live in the central registry, separate scheme
   fragments, or both with one generated representation.
5. How a scheme override interacts with an explicit legacy =personality= when
   no registered semantic role is present.
6. Whether content speech is an action or a distinguished content capability
   in the final data model.
7. The safe persistent storage format and migration mechanism.
8. The exact context snapshot boundary around =tts-speak= and notification
   speech.
9. Serialization and timing when speech and cues use different processes.
10. Pulse/PipeWire sample naming and cleanup for contextual assets.
11. The portable spatial coordinate ranges and how 3D values reduce to stereo.
12. Whether critical alerts may be completely suppressed or require an
    alternative presentation.
13. The ordering policy required before minor-mode selectors are enabled.

Initial recommendations are:

- use versioned declarative Lisp data with no evaluation;
- resolve all context and resources in Emacs before queueing;
- treat existing personalities as presentation hints, not semantic identity;
- make stereo balance the portable spatial baseline;
- use explicit action operations rather than implicit last-writer behavior;
- warn once per session for unknown legacy inputs during migration.

* Risks

- Calling everything an icon would preserve a sound-first design and make
  speech or voice alternatives second-class.
- Treating visual faces as semantic identity would couple accessibility
  behavior to display styling and package implementation details.
- A rule engine without explicit composition would make independently useful
  facts such as heading level and folded state conflict.
- Context inferred after text enters the TTS scratch buffer would select the
  wrong mode or buffer rules.
- Backend-specific semantic resolution would make presentation depend on the
  selected player.
- Queueing unresolved cue names would push Emacs configuration and fallback
  policy into the speech server.
- Runtime spatialization of the existing =3d= assets could position them
  twice.
- Unstable or excessive spatial speech could reduce intelligibility.
- Requiring every scheme to cover every extension would make third-party
  schemes impractical.
- An editor that cannot explain rule provenance would make a powerful cascade
  impossible to debug.
- Migrating every caller at once would obscure semantic review and make
  regressions difficult to isolate.

The staged plan addresses these risks with a registered semantic vocabulary, a
pure and explainable rule engine, concrete pre-queue resolution, capability
fallback, an Org vertical slice, and small subsequent migrations.

* Definition of Done

The migration is complete when:

- registered semantic facts are the documented source of truth;
- modules describe meaning without choosing a modality or file;
- schemes can compose speech, voice, cues, pauses, and spatial style in
  explicit before, content, and after phases;
- users can inspect and override presentation globally and by module, major
  mode, combined context, derived mode, or buffer;
- independent rules such as heading level and folded state compose
  deterministically;
- existing ACSS personalities and sound packs are providers within the common
  system;
- the concrete render plan is resolved before queueing;
- the speech server contains no scheme, mode, module, or semantic resolution;
- supported backends honor the same ordering, silence, and fallback behavior;
- portable spatial presentation degrades predictably and can be disabled;
- training and explanation commands identify what was presented and why;
- schemes, resource packs, voice palettes, and semantic extensions validate;
- existing icon calls, =personality= properties, face mappings, and bundled
  directories remain functional through documented compatibility adapters;
- the Org vertical slice and representative module migrations pass all
  required test gates;
- public documentation explains authoring, customization, migration, and
  backend capability behavior.
