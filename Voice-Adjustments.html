6.6.1 Understand Voice Adjustments ¶

Change one field at a time and compare with B. These are portable controls, not measurements in Hertz or decibels. Equal numbers can sound different on different engines. Most numeric fields use integer levels from zero through nine; Relative Rate is the exception.

  • Relative Rate ranges from -20 to 20. Negative values request slower speech, positive values faster speech, and zero leaves the base rate unchanged. The engine interprets those relative steps; they are not words per minute. A stronger contextual relative-rate value replaces this value rather than adding to it.
  • Average Pitch requests a lower or higher overall pitch. Start with a small difference between two voices intended to mark related content.
  • Pitch Range requests less or more pitch variation. A low range can make a supported voice more monotone; a high range makes intonation more varied.
  • Stress requests less or more emphasis. Its audible effect depends on how the engine implements stress.
  • Richness changes the voice’s tonal character or fullness where supported. Compare samples rather than treating it as another volume control.
  • Gain changes output level. Five is unchanged; lower values reduce it and higher values increase it.
  • Pan places supported audio from left at zero, through center at five, to right at nine. Its effect also depends on your output arrangement.
  • Low-pass Amount and High-pass Amount remove high and low frequencies, respectively. Both use zero for neutral and nine for strongest filtering.
  • Reverb adds a reverberant character, Echo adds delayed repetitions, and Chorus adds a doubled-voice effect. Zero disables each; higher levels increase the effect.
  • Portable Fallback Family, where offered under additional adjustments, is a compatibility family hint. Use Physical voice for an installed voice choice. Family is not part of the complete Omnivox voice preview.

In shared settings, d or blank numeric input requests adapter default; u undoes that edit. In an individual physical choice, i instead means use the shared value. Zero remains an explicit value in either view. Thus “standard default palette”, “Automatic physical selection”, and “adapter default adjustment” answer three different questions: which collection supplies the voice, who selects its physical voice, and who supplies one adjustment value.

The voice editor presents Low-pass Amount and High-pass Amount from zero through nine. Higher amounts mean more filtering: low-pass removes more high frequencies, and high-pass removes more low frequencies. Zero is the neutral cutoff; blank numeric entry requests the adapter default. The amount scale applies to arrows and numeric entry in the common editor. Existing saved styles retain their sound; stored low-pass cutoff values keep their original meaning.

The voice editor names other neutral effect values instead of speaking an ambiguous number: gain five is unchanged, pan five is centre, and reverb, echo, or chorus zero is disabled. Chorus adds a bounded doubled-voice modulation without changing speech duration or marker positions. It is applied by Omnivox to buffered audio; the voice editor reports when the selected route cannot apply it. These portable levels are translated to the exact values required by the active adapter.

The built-in acss-default palette also includes a telephone named voice. It combines high-pass and low-pass filtering to give any buffered Omnivox engine a narrow-band telephone sound while preserving its selected physical voice, duration, and markers. Preview it in Voice Palettes or Voice Workbench before assigning it to content. Adapters without post-synthesis filtering preserve the portable style but do not apply the filters.

Omnivox average-pitch differences use the customizable ‘omnivox-average-pitch-contrast’. Its default value, ‘0.5’, keeps palette distinctions while making them gentler. Set it to ‘1.0’ for the historical contrast, or ‘0’ to make average pitch neutral. Values through ‘2.0’ are accepted. Changing it refreshes logical-voice registration, and the same setting applies to previews, structured speech, and legacy inline voices.
