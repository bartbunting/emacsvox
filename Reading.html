5.5 Reading And Navigating ¶

Choose the amount of text you need to hear. Use a line or paragraph for a local check, a selected region for exact editing boundaries, or C-e i for longer reading with a tracked position. Ordinary movement speaks what you reach; explicit inspection does not require that movement first.

The default keys are collected here. The sections below explain which preserve point, how to stop and start again, and the limits of large requests. These distinctions matter before you begin selecting text.

C-e c ¶

emacsvox-speak-char speaks the character at point phonetically.

C-e w ¶

emacsvox-speak-word speaks the current word. Repeating it at the same position spells the word.

C-e l ¶

emacsvox-speak-line speaks the current line.

C-e [ ¶

emacsvox-speak-paragraph speaks the current paragraph.

C-e ] ¶

emacsvox-speak-page speaks the current page.

C-e r ¶

emacsvox-speak-region speaks the region between point and mark. Establish and check the selection before editing; large requests have the windowful limit explained below.

C-e i ¶

emacsvox-speak-rest-of-buffer reads from point towards the buffer end with chunk-based position tracking. It requires a supporting server; see Read from point, stop, and start again.

C-e b ¶

emacsvox-speak-buffer reads the accessible buffer without tracking point through the speech. Large requests fall back to windowful reading, as explained below.

C-e s ¶

tts-stop stops current speech.

C-e C-d ¶

emacsvox-toggle-show-point toggles the aural indication of point within spoken text.

Many reading commands accept a prefix to limit speech before or after point. Use C-h k before relying on a variant; exact prefix behaviour belongs to that command’s documentation.

5.5.1 Choose source lines or wrapped screen rows ¶

A source line ends at a newline. A long source line can occupy several screen rows, with the breaks changing when you resize the window. Open C-e H s R w, Line reading and wrapping, or M-x emacsvox-reading-settings RET to choose which unit ordinary Up and Down move through and speak. Whole source lines reads the entire source line. Wrapped screen rows enables Visual Line mode and reads the displayed row. Native mode behaviour preserves the major mode’s own defaults. Tables, terminals and other integrations can own their navigation and keep their specialised behaviour.

Ordinary long prose does not ask to change this choice. Unusually large lines and long lines containing control bytes ask before speaking; declining leaves the reading mode unchanged. C-e l remains a one-shot whole-source-line inspection in either reading mode. For an occasional position check, use = in the menu or M-x emacsvox-speak-row-position RET. It reports the source line number and the screen row’s position within it without moving point. The source buffer must be displayed so Emacs can determine its row boundaries.

Wrapped rows can identify their place in the source line. A first row has more text below, a middle row has text above and below, and a last row has text above. A complete source line has no continuation cue. The menu’s 1, 2 and 3 open independent editors for these three types. Each offers Off, Tone or Recorded earcon, its own sound identity and volume, and Before, Alongside or After speech. Before finishes the sound first; Alongside starts it with the spoken item; After waits for that item to finish. Navigation or Stop cancels remaining feedback, including an After sound.

The initial wrapped-row choices are three distinct recorded cues alongside speech. t stages three distinct tones instead; each row’s tone editor controls pitch, duration and shaping while retaining the chosen sound type. Apply or Save in that editor updates the parent draft. P and V stage a common placement or volume while keeping the three sound identities. Each row’s recording selector chooses a cue from the active sound pack. Its library action lets you audition recordings and explicitly assign them to wrapped-line-first, wrapped-line-middle or wrapped-line-last in a personal sound pack. The library’s W saves pack-wide assignments separately from reading preferences; it returns to the reading draft when you close the library.

Spoken continuation is independent of sound. Choose Off, Brief labels, or Explicit descriptions of continuation above and below. Descriptions precede the row text, and sound timing applies to that complete spoken item. Frequency can be every wrapped-row visit or only entry into a different source line, including a jump into its middle. Changing reading buffers or windows resets entry tracking. Explicit screen-row rereading can repeat the feedback.

Choices edit a draft. Browsing does not apply them. Start in temporary buffer scope, or use g to choose the current major mode or global defaults. Each preference follows buffer, mode, global and factory values in that order. Apply with a for this session; S saves and applies mode or global choices through Customize. Buffer scope is not saved per file. Applying wider settings preserves explicit buffer overrides. i stages inheritance, r stages factory values, and d discards unapplied edits. Leaving keeps applied changes and discards unapplied edits. Apply or discard before changing scope.

Use p to preview a complete line followed by first, middle and last rows, or Space in an individual editor to preview one type. These previews use drafts without applying them; c compares the source’s applied settings and x stops playback. Help at h shows applied values, inheritance and worker support. Sounds require structured timeline support, and recorded earcons follow the auditory-icon switch. Unsupported sounds, volume or shaping are identified without silently changing their placement. Listening to the previews helps you choose levels that leave the words clear.
