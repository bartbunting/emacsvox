6.7 Choose Voices And Speech Engines ¶

Four terms help when adjusting voices:

  • A logical voice expresses a purpose such as boldening, annotation, or a heading distinction without naming one synthesizer voice.
  • A voice palette gives named logical voices portable style characteristics. Palettes can inherit from another palette and travel between machines.
  • A physical voice is an actual voice reported by an installed speech engine. Its exact identifier is usually machine-local.
  • An engine route decides which engine and physical voice realize a logical voice on the current machine.

Use the Voice Palettes manager when you want to choose or edit the portable style vocabulary. Press RET on a palette to browse its voices, use P to audition the palette sequence before editing, and use a in the palette manager to activate a selected palette. Built-in palettes remain intact; editing can create a personal copy.

With Voice Lock enabled, named formatting voices such as bolden and annotate follow the active palette in search feedback, completion, status speech, and the named-voice sampler. Explicit aural rules can override these formatting voices.

If you assign voices in your personal Lisp configuration, use a name such as 'voice-bolden to follow the palette. For example: 

(setq emacsvox-calendar-mark-personality 'voice-bolden)

Legacy settings such as voice-bolden-settings still supply the fallback when the palette has no custom definition. Explicit ACSS styles retain their own settings. Restart Emacs after updating Emacsvox to load changed voice defaults; explicit choices in your personal configuration remain in effect.

Use Voice Workbench when the question involves installed voices, engines, or routes. Open it from Aural Home with W. Its main views are:

l

Logical voices and their current routes.

v

Physical voices reported by the engines.

e

Engines, preference, fallback, and health.

s

Portable styles and effects.

For physical voices, choose Browse and try voices in Home, then press RET on an engine to browse its voices. RET on a voice shows details; P plays a named sample. Filters and each view’s selected row and column survive navigation through Home. Press q in an engine’s voice list to return to that engine’s row and column. Press q again from the engine list to leave the workbench. The direct view shortcuts remain available.

The engine list reports Availability before routing preferences. An engine with a missing runtime stays visible as unavailable, with its reason under Last failure. Routing policy: allowed means the policy permits using that engine; it does not mean its runtime is installed. Disabled policies, health, and temporary failures are reported separately.

A plays available matching voices in displayed order, skipping unavailable rows. B starts with the selected voice as A and offers another matching voice as B. Choose Search all engines inside that prompt to compare outside the current filters. Both samples use the same sentence. T edits it, F filters, C clears filters, and R refreshes inventory.

Labels identify each engine and voice. Customize emacsvox-aural-preview-label-verbosity for names, brief letters or numbers, or no labels. Labels and samples share one preview sequence: S or foreground navigation cancels the remaining samples. An exact-preview failure is reported instead of accepting a different voice as the requested one. Availability and capability reports describe what the adapter advertises; they are not a listening test of every parameter.

Use a to assign a physical voice and t to tune the selected logical voice on its effective route. Route changes are staged until you press w or C-c C-c to save and apply them. Returning to the engine list preserves staged route changes. Leaving the workbench with q warns when those changes remain unsaved.

Applying waits for each speech stream to acknowledge its configuration. If an Omnivox apply times out, the Workbench reports server outcome unconfirmed. Emacsvox stops that apply and sends no later steps, but changes already sent may still take effect on the server. A timeout does not undo the saved profile or confirm that the server rejected it. Press r to reapply the committed profile and obtain a fresh result. Save or cancel any newer staged edits before retrying. A partial result retains the streams that acknowledged success and identifies those that timed out.

The tuner names neutral effect values instead of speaking an ambiguous number: gain five is unchanged, low-pass nine is disabled, high-pass zero is disabled, pan five is centre, and reverb, echo, or chorus zero is disabled. Chorus adds a bounded doubled-voice modulation without changing speech duration or marker positions. It is applied by Omnivox to buffered audio; the tuner reports when the selected route cannot apply it. These portable levels are translated to the exact values required by the active adapter.

The built-in acss-default palette also includes a telephone logical voice. It combines high-pass and low-pass filtering to give any buffered Omnivox engine a narrow-band telephone sound while preserving its selected physical voice, duration, and markers. Preview it in Voice Palettes or Voice Workbench before assigning it to content. Adapters without post-synthesis filtering preserve the portable style but do not apply the filters.

Omnivox average-pitch differences use the customizable ‘omnivox-average-pitch-contrast’. Its default value, ‘0.5’, keeps palette distinctions while making them gentler. Set it to ‘1.0’ for the historical contrast, or ‘0’ to make average pitch neutral. Values through ‘2.0’ are accepted. Changing it refreshes logical-voice registration, and the same setting applies to previews, structured speech, and legacy inline voices.

For a quick engine preference, C-e d e chooses an available engine for the current Emacs session. Press C-u first to save the new preference. On a first run with no saved routing profile, the ordinary command uses the live Omnivox policy as an in-memory baseline and writes no file; the prefixed command explicitly saves that baseline and the selected preference. Explicit logical-voice routes still take precedence.

Judge voices in context. The goal is usually to distinguish nearby kinds of content, not to identify every named voice in isolation. Use a portable palette for the intended distinction and machine routing for the best local realization.

6.7.1 Tune A Physical Voice Temporarily ¶

In Browse and try voices, select a physical voice and press t. This opens a temporary experiment with that exact engine and voice, engine-default speech characteristics, the current speech rate, and neutral audio effects. Opening, adjusting, comparing, and cancelling perform no configuration writes or route assignments.

The experiment uses its working voice for navigation, values, and Help. n/p or up/down selects a parameter; left/right adjusts it; RET enters a value; blank retains the current value and d explicitly requests the adapter default. P plays the working version. B alternates the opening and working versions, u undoes, R restores the opening voice and parameters, and S stops. T changes the sample, c copies an existing style, and v tries another physical voice while retaining the requested parameters.

For a three-value comparison, select a supported numeric parameter and choose Demonstrate the selected parameter from C-c C-a, or press D. Enter three valid values; each is labelled before the identical sentence. This demonstration does not change the working settings and can be stopped as one sequence.

Engine speech controls and Omnivox audio effects are identified separately. The Requested column shows your settings. Playback reports show omissions or acceptance; adapters do not report exact effective numeric values. A failed physical voice is reported explicitly. If it prevents navigation, ordinary speech keeps the tuner operable and announces that fallback.

Press w or C-c C-c to choose Keep result: parameters as a named style, a physical voice route retaining the existing style, or both voice and parameters for a named logical voice. A built-in palette is copied only when you save the reviewed result. The review names the destinations and shows the parameters. Press C-c C-c in that review to save and apply them. Palette selection applies for the session; a Presentation Profile can retain that choice. Route and style files remain separate. Any partial save or failed apply is reported, and the review retains completed steps so you can retry without repeating writes. q returns from the review; cancelling before Save and apply leaves configuration unchanged.

6.7.2 Worked Presentation Recipes ¶

  1. Browse Eloquence And Compare Pitch
    1. Open C-e H and search with / for Browse and try voices. Select the installed Eloquence engine and press RET. Its list shows the voices this installation reports; if Eloquence is unavailable, choose another available engine.
    2. Use up/down to choose a voice. P plays it; A plays the filtered list with spoken names, and S stops. T chooses your own sample sentence. For two different voices, B uses the selected voice as A and asks for B.
    3. Press t on one physical voice. Move to Average Pitch. Press RET and enter 3, then P. Enter 7 and replay the same sentence. u restores the previous value for another comparison. Navigation also uses the working voice, so you can hear it in actual use.
    4. For a labelled sequence, press D on Average Pitch and supply three valid values, for example 3, 5, and 7. This does not change the working value. B compares the opening and working versions; it does not compare the two most recent edits.
  2. Keep A Voice Experiment

    From the physical tuner, press w after finding a useful setting. Choose parameters as a named style to keep characteristics while letting existing routes choose the physical voice; choose physical voice route to assign that voice while retaining the destination’s existing style; choose both to keep both for the chosen logical voice.

    Choose a destination name and, when needed, an editable palette. Read the review, including the exact physical route and parameter values, and use P to preview that proposal. Only C-c C-c in the review saves and applies it. Palette selection is temporary for this session; save a Presentation Profile if you want to retain that selection. The route has its own machine-local saved profile. If a step fails, the review states which writes succeeded and retains the remaining steps for retry. q before saving changes nothing.

  3. Change A Heading Voice, Add A Cue, Or Silence One

    Move to a representative heading and open C-e H c. Press c, choose Change the content voice, and select a named voice such as bolden. P previews it. Use m to review the actual heading facts, including level when supplied by the integration. Use l to choose a lifetime, review the summary, then press a. A rule matching level-two headings affects all matching level-two headings within that lifetime’s scope.

    To add a cue, begin again with a representative item and choose Add a sound. Select before or after content, choose a registered sound, and preview it. To silence an existing cue, choose Suppress one component and select that sound by phase and identity. This retains the spoken content and other feedback. Review matching criteria and lifetime before applying either rule.

  4. Change A Blank-Line Tone

    Navigate onto a blank line so its feedback enters history. Open C-e H H, select that blank-line record, and press C. Choose Change a tone, select the tone, then choose a named tone or explicit pitch in Hertz and duration in milliseconds. Compare O and P. Review the matching facts before choosing lifetime and applying.

    If that record contains a cue instead of a tone, the tone choice is absent. Use Explain to see which rule supplied the cue; a guided change can suppress that cue, and a second change can add a tone. The interface reports the actual presentation rather than assuming every visually blank line uses the same feedback.

  5. Choose An Engine And Recover Unfinished Work

    For ordinary speech, open Home and search for Ordinary speech engine, or use C-e d e directly. Choose an available engine. This preference lasts for the session; a prefix argument explicitly saves it. Existing exact logical-voice routes take precedence over the preference.

    Use h to leave a working editor through Home without losing it. Home’s Resume unfinished changes opens the original draft, including its source and working settings. In a voice tuner, u undoes and R restores the opening settings. Its q asks before discarding changes. Guided feedback q hides and retains the draft. To undo an already applied rule, open Presentation Overrides, select it in the appropriate scope, and use t to disable it or d to remove it.

    From any Aural interface, C-c C-i opens its offline task manual and q returns to the same interface and position. In Help, i opens that manual. C-c C-a offers named actions; S stops a preview sequence.
