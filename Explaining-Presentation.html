6.3 Understand Why Something Sounded That Way ¶

Use this workflow when feedback is surprising, unclear, or especially useful:

  1. Leave point on the object and press C-e E, or choose Feedback for this item in Aural Home. Both open Feedback Details with a brief announcement rather than speaking the complete explanation.
  2. Read the explanation as a report of the object and occasion, matching presentation rules, selected actions, concrete voices and sounds, and any backend fallback or degradation.
  3. Note the strongest rule that changed the part you care about. This tells you whether to inspect an option, a personal or temporary rule, a named voice, a sound pack, or the Voice Lock compatibility layer.

When a retained presentation still matches the source buffer, position, and unchanged source text, the explanation describes the exact output that was queued, including its source location and resolved resources. It does not reinterpret that old output using your latest settings. Without an exact record, Emacsvox labels the explanation as a simulation of what the current rules would produce at point. The simulation uses captured source text and context; Play simulation plays that baseline, not a recording of something you previously heard. It supports the same field review and draft previews as recorded feedback, and is never added to Recent Feedback. Use a prefix argument with C-e E to choose a simulated occasion explicitly. Press q to return to the source position or Home row from which you opened the report.

Expand a field and choose Edit named voice bolden (or the voice name shown) beside its voice explanation to open the existing voice editor. A field with several named voices offers a link for each. This edits the named voice in the reported palette, affecting every use there. Change field instead changes the feedback rule for that field. Leaving the voice editor returns to the same report link; opening it does not save or apply anything.

To inspect something heard earlier, open Aural Home and choose Recent feedback, or press H there. Each row preserves a recorded presentation; the history has a size limit. Moving through the Content column speaks the preview using its recorded voices, including voice changes within an entry. Column titles and the other fields use the normal interface voice. Useful commands in the feedback browser include:

RET or e

Open a readable Feedback Details buffer for the selected presentation.

P

Replay its complete ordered presentation without adding a history entry.

c

Audition only its auditory cues.

r

Prepare a voice remap from the selected record.

R

Replace, suppress, or restore one exact earcon.

L

Change the bounded history limit for the current session.

i

Include or exclude feedback from the Aural interfaces themselves.

Aural interface feedback is excluded by default so it does not displace the feedback you are investigating. This includes prompts opened from an Aural screen, their completion choices, and their opening and closing sounds. Ordinary completion outside these screens remains eligible for history. The setting controls new recordings; it does not remove entries already retained.

Feedback Details selects a separate, read-only buffer and announces only a brief introduction. Its report stays attached to that recorded presentation while new feedback arrives. Ordinary Help commands do not replace the report. Press q to return to the same row and column in Recent Feedback.

The report groups consecutive voice spans into semantic fields where the integration supplies them. In a Notmuch search result, for example, you can visit Authors, Subject, Date, Message count, and Tags. Headings identify the recorded voices. Collapsed fields show only their headings. Visibility is conveyed through the selected aural presentation, using earcons or optional spoken labels; the words expanded and collapsed are not inserted into the heading text. Press RET on a heading to reveal its text, playback and change controls, voice source, sounds, and any backend limitations. Field content is read in its recorded voices. Only fields with multiple spans show separate span sections, behind Individual voice spans. Expand that section only when you want to inspect or change an individual span. Its heading announces the section without repeating the field’s content. A single-span field shows its text and controls once, and its heading omits the span count. Press RET on the field heading again to collapse it.

Fields with unapplied drafts announce unsaved change after their names. The report also shows how many fields have unsaved changes. These labels update as you edit or apply a change, preserving the report’s buttons and your return position. g updates draft status, for example after killing a draft buffer. The original or simulated presentation remains the same.

n or p

Move between headings. Collapsed fields speak only their heading; expanded fields also speak their content in the recorded voices.

Up or Down

Review individual lines without speaking the complete report.

TAB or Shift-TAB

Move between action buttons; RET activates an action or expands a field.

O

Play the original field or span at point, or its labelled simulated baseline.

P

Play the complete original presentation, or the labelled simulation.

C

Open or resume a change to the selected field or distinguishable span.

r

Choose another named voice for matching items in the compact editor.

R

Replace, suppress, or restore an exact sound in the selected field or span.

V

Preview the complete presentation with all linked draft changes.

S

Stop playback.

g

Update the report’s draft status.

For example, select Subject, press C, and choose another content voice. In that editor, O plays the original field and P previews the changed field. For a complete comparison, choose Play whole original or press B, then use V to preview the whole presentation with all linked drafts. Both actions keep you in the change buffer. The original stays frozen even after you change a draft; it can be played before choosing a change or lifetime. For simulated feedback, Play whole simulation plays the frozen simulated baseline instead. Return to the report and select Message count to add a sound After content. The sound is placed once after the complete count, even when that field contains several voices. The subject and count editors retain their drafts separately. Their matching criteria identify the field, integration, mode, and occasion; inspect those criteria before choosing a buffer, session, or saved personal lifetime. Changing a field’s voice mapping does not retune the shared named voice.

Choose another voice for matching items opens the compact voice editor for that field. Its Applies to row identifies the matching items; choose How long before saving. Change field opens the full component editor and resumes the same draft. If the draft already changes a sound or another component, the full editor stays visible so that change is not hidden.

Sound overrides: replace, suppress, or restore uses the existing exact-sound workflow, limited to the selected field. It opens a separate rule draft for review and saving; that draft is not included in the report’s linked-field preview. Restore inherited behavior removes an override made by this sound workflow in the selected scope. Press q to return to the report.

Original playback uses retained output; a simulated report labels its baseline playback as simulated throughout the report and linked change editor. Proposed playback uses current rules, captured buffer context, and the linked drafts without applying them. A span can be targeted separately only when its semantic field, a distinct named face, or an explicit voice annotation identifies it. Truncated history cannot provide complete playback or a comparable change preview. Older records missing preview inputs remain readable and replayable when complete; navigate over the source again to capture a new example before previewing changes.

Each expanded field explains where its voice and sounds came from, such as a visual face or your personal override. These explanations use the captured sources that actually contributed to that field. Limitations, such as an unsupported voice family, appear beside the affected field.

Debug details opens the raw snapshot in a separate read-only buffer for troubleshooting. It contains all recorded facts, rule identifiers, resolved voice properties, and backend diagnostics. Opening it speaks only a brief announcement. Press q to return to the same place in Feedback Details; the report keeps its expanded fields and linked drafts.

Explain feedback as you navigate, available from Aural Home, speaks concise semantic explanations after normal presentations. It can help while learning the vocabulary used by an integration, but is intentionally more verbose than ordinary operation; turn it off when you no longer need the additional labels.
