6.2 What You Can Hear ¶

Aural Presentation can use one or more of these forms of feedback:

  • Speech conveys content, labels, prompts, results, and status. Added speech should explain meaning that is not already clear from the content.
  • Voice changes characteristics of spoken content. Named logical voices make distinctions such as headings, annotations, warnings, or quoted text portable across speech engines.
  • Auditory cues are short recognizable sounds associated with semantic events or objects. A sound pack supplies the concrete audio for those cue names.
  • Tones convey compact information using a named pitch and duration. They are useful when a short distinction is preferable to speech or a sampled sound.
  • Pauses separate related parts of a presentation without adding words.
  • Spatial presentation can place speech or cues across supported output. When the selected backend cannot provide the requested placement, Emacsvox keeps the content audible using its documented fallback.

These forms can be ordered around the content. For example, entering a section might play a cue, speak the heading in a distinctive voice, and leave a short pause before body text. The presentation is one result, not several unrelated notifications.

The precise sound may vary by speech engine, installed voices, sound pack, and audio hardware. The intended distinction should remain understandable even when a simpler backend uses fewer voice characteristics or centered output.
