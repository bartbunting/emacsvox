6.4.3 Remap One Item At Point ¶

  1. Move to a representative object and press C-e E so you know what currently controls it.
  2. Press C-e H. Choose Choose a voice for this item with r to use a different named voice here, or Edit the named voice used here with T to open the current named voice in the common voice editor. Tuning changes that voice wherever it is used in the palette; remapping changes which voice is used for the face or item. For example, at a shell prompt you can assign another voice to the prompt face, or tune the voice currently used by prompts. You do not need to hear the prompt first.

    In the remapping screen, move with n and p or the arrow keys. Press RET on Voice to choose a voice, Preview original to hear the captured item, or Preview change to hear your choice. O and P also play these previews; S stops. Previewing does not save the mapping or retune the named voice.

    R in Home still remaps one exact earcon.

  3. Choose How long deliberately:
    • a personal rule persists across Emacs sessions;
    • a session rule lasts until this Emacs exits; and
    • a buffer rule affects only the remembered live buffer.
  4. Check Applies to, Voice, and How long, then activate Save or press w. A face mapping affects matching uses of that face within the displayed module or mode and chosen lifetime. Advanced opens the full rule editor when you need more detailed matching controls. At the top level, q hides the simple remapping screen and keeps its draft; Resume unfinished changes in Home finds it again after a voice is chosen.
  5. Return to the source, repeat the action, and use C-e E again to confirm which rule now wins.
