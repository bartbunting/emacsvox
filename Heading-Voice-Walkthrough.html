6.4.1 Walkthrough: Change Headings In One Buffer ¶

This example changes headings in a temporary Org buffer. It leaves your palette definitions and saved rules alone.

  1. Use C-x b to create a new buffer named *aural-practice*. Run M-x org-mode and insert this short document: 
    * Reading notes
An ordinary paragraph.
* Next steps
Another ordinary paragraph.
  2. Move onto the first heading and press C-e E. Find its current named voice and the facts identifying a heading. The explanation should tell you whether it describes recorded output or a simulation.
  3. Open C-e H c. Press c, select Content voice, and choose annotate with RET. If annotate is already the original, use bolden instead. O plays the original and P previews your choice. At this point the change exists only in the draft.
  4. Press m to inspect What should match. Keep the Org module and heading role, plus heading level if shown. Leave other criteria as shown for this first example. Press l and choose Current buffer, until it is killed. This means every matching heading in this buffer, not just the heading you started from.
  5. Review the summary, then press a. Return through Home with h, then C-c C-o. Move between the two headings using the same navigation action. Both matching headings should use your chosen voice; the ordinary paragraphs should keep their previous presentation.
  6. Press C-e E on a heading again. Check that the new buffer rule supplies the selected voice. If the sound is too similar to distinguish, the explanation still tells you whether the rule matched; compare voices in the palette or consult Troubleshooting.
  7. To undo, open Home’s Your feedback rules with O. Find the new rule in the buffer scope and inspect its target. Press t to disable it or d to remove it, then revisit the heading. Killing this practice buffer also ends its buffer rules. Nothing from this example survives a restart.

For a permanent change, repeat the task on a representative item and choose Saved personal rule at step 4. Review the matching criteria carefully: a permanent rule can affect future matching buffers too.
