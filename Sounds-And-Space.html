8.10 Sounds, Tones, And Spatial Cues ¶

Rules request cues by name. Sound packs supply recordings for those names and can inherit missing cues. This lets you change the sound of a family of cues without rewriting rules. A recording library holds available sounds; assigning one to a cue changes its sound in that pack. To change when the cue plays, use its feedback rule.

To enable or disable earcons in the captured buffer, use C-e H r l e. In that submenu, E changes the global default for buffers without a local override; i removes the source buffer’s override so it follows that default. Speech remains available when earcons are off, but menu cues also disappear where the off setting applies. These switches last for the session or buffer and have no Customize Save action. Toggle again or restore inheritance to reverse an experiment.

C-e H o p opens Sound Packs. Its q returns to Sounds and output with the same settings scope, and another q returns to the main menu. Directly opened browsers keep their usual returns.

Audition a representative cue without activating the pack.

Browse individual cues and their source or fallback.

Audition the selected concrete cue; RET shows details.

Validate resources and report missing/invalid entries.

Spatial requests describe placement portably, not a particular device. C-e H D s reports capabilities and fallbacks; C-e H o n controls output, separation, direction, speech, and cues. Unsupported or mono output retains audible content at the centre. Named tones are separate compact resources, independent of sampled files and the active pack.

For waveform, pitch sweep and envelope adjustments, use the shared tone editor. Spelling, capitalisation, indentation, wrapped rows, line conditions and tree levels open it from their respective settings. Its controls are shared, while each destination has its own scope and saving rules. For tree entry, exit and depth feedback, follow Read and customise text trees. General personal-rule editing retains its own controls.

8.10.1 Tune line and boundary cues from the menu ¶

C-e H o c opens Line and boundary cues. Blank, whitespace-only, separator, decorated, and unspeakable-line tones have pitch and integer-duration controls. Shift-left/right adjusts by 25 Hz or 5 milliseconds; RET enters exactly. Their E opens the shared tone editor for volume, waveform, sweep and envelope. Boundary/warning and large movement instead choose replacement earcons. Changing those named earcons affects every use, including warnings elsewhere.

Each cue retains a global draft. SPC auditions it, s stops, n silences in the draft, r restores bundled values, and D discards unapplied edits. A applies a temporary session rule. S saves the conditional personal rule and removes its temporary session override. Unrelated rules remain intact and other presentation rules may override the result. To undo an applied change, choose the desired values or bundled restoration, then Apply or Save again.

Inside the shared editor, A applies for the session and W saves the personal rule. Preview leaves the parent’s draft and live rules unchanged; q discards unapplied editor adjustments and returns to the same Edit tone control. The parent menu’s q retains its draft, including an applied change. Line cues use the presentation-rule store because they match line conditions. These controls change cue output, not classification of separators or decorated lines.
