6.6 Edit A Named Voice ¶

From Voice Palettes, open a palette, select a voice, and press e to edit it. From Aural Home, Edit the named voice used here opens the named voice used by the captured source item. Editing an inherited voice creates a complete entry in your destination palette when saved. Editing a built-in palette asks for a personal child. Opening the editor and previewing a draft do not change live feedback or save settings.

If you opened an experiment from an installed physical voice, these same controls apply, but saving first asks for a destination. See Keep an Installed Voice Experiment for that sequence.

The editor uses the ordinary navigation voice for its labelled fields. n/p, up/down or Tab moves between fields. Left/right adjusts a numeric field and moves between fields elsewhere; RET activates a button or enters an adjustment value. Press d on an adjustment to restore the adapter default; blank numeric entry does the same. Zero is an explicit value, such as the lowest pitch or leftmost pan, whereas adapter default leaves that setting to the speech adapter. They may sound the same for some effects, but they are different settings. More adjustments and effects exposes filters, gain, pan and other optional controls. Changed values say what they were before editing; unsupported settings remain visible and are retained. Field navigation stops at the first and last field without wrapping. Numeric adjustments stop at their limits and announce the minimum or maximum.

P plays the edited voice. B compares the captured original with the edited version using the same text, with spoken labels. T changes that text; S stops. An automatic sample follows each adjustment unless you turn off Automatic sample after adjustment. The new value precedes the sample text, for example “1. The quick brown fox…”. Toggling Automatic sample after adjustment announces on or off with distinct cues. More adjustments and effects and Fallbacks present their visibility with open/close cues and optional spoken state labels, both when toggled and when reached during field navigation. Navigation stops an outstanding sample. u undoes an edit, including a reset to adapter default. q dismisses the editor and returns to its originating view, offering to retain or discard unsaved changes.

Choose the Physical voice field to search by engine or voice name. You can audition a candidate with the working adjustments before accepting it. Cancel keeps the previous choice and returns to the same field. Named voices also have an expandable Fallbacks section: replace, reorder, remove or individually audition a choice, append another, or explicitly return to Automatic. Existing unavailable, engine-default and property choices are preserved. Shared adjustments apply to fields that a row inherits; Customize this voice adds optional individual settings.

For a named voice, w or C-c C-c saves and applies the complete draft. Save to collection without selecting saves an inactive palette without changing the current selection. The header reports unsaved changes, partial saves, applying, failures, or saved and applied. Retry uses the pending proposal and retains successful persistence steps. Changes made during an earlier apply remain unsaved when its acknowledgement arrives. A changed file or destination requires fresh review rather than an implicit merge.

Named-voice previews test the complete choice chain without selecting the draft palette or changing live settings. They require an Omnivox server advertising complete-voice preview. Older servers report this operation as unsupported; individual physical auditions remain available. Playback reports identify actual accepted audio when the server supplies that evidence, including unsupported adjustments. They do not infer a successful fallback from inventory or claim exact effective numeric parameter values.

q returns to the originating row and column. With unsaved changes, choose to retain the draft, discard changes, or continue editing. Retained drafts survive closing the view and can be reopened from Home’s Resume unfinished changes during this Emacs session. They do not survive restarting Emacs. Leaving does not undo a completed save.

Save and apply selects the palette for this session. Use the Use at Startup action, U in Voice Palettes, to retain that selection after restart. A save can involve more than one settings file. The editor reports partial failures and retains the proposal for retry; see Recovering Unfinished Changes.
