13.4 Reviewing The Term Screen ¶

The review pointer is independent of the terminal cursor. Moving it does not send keys or move the child application. It reviews the Term buffer, not the immutable snapshot used by EAT’s frozen review. The normal prefix is C-t:

C-t .

Place the review pointer at the terminal cursor and announce its coordinates.

C-t p
C-t n

Move to the previous or next line and speak it. C-t <up> and C-t <down> are equivalent.

C-t <left>
C-t <right>

Move by a character and speak the destination.

C-t M-b
C-t M-f

Move backward or forward by a word and speak it.

C-t l
C-t w
C-t c

Read the line, word, or character at the review pointer without moving.

C-t SPC

Read from the pointer to the current end of the Term buffer.

C-t s

Ask for text, search forward from the pointer, and read the matching line.

In character mode, C-t q enters dedicated review. Ordinary keys then stay in review rather than reaching the child. Use p, n, arrows, M-b, M-f, l, w, c, and s without the prefix. q returns to character mode. To copy text, set one endpoint with m, move the pointer, then press y to copy the region to the kill ring.

Named rectangular regions and focus/filter windows are advanced, coordinate-based facilities. Consult the live mode Help and generated reference before using them; remember the character-mode Help prefix when ordinary Emacs keys would otherwise reach the child.
