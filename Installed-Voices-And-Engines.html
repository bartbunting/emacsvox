6.7 Browse Installed Voices And Choose Engines ¶

Use Voice Workbench when the question involves installed voices or engines. Open it from Aural Home with W. Its main views are:

e

Engines, such as Flite or Eloquence. Start here to try voices from a particular synthesizer or inspect its availability.

v

Physical voices: the actual installed voices you can hear. Use this list to listen, compare, and experiment with their parameters. On an engine row, this key opens just that engine’s voices.

l

Named voices (the Logical voices view): roles such as bolden and annotate. Start here to change which physical voice reads a role.

s

Styles and effects for those named voices. Use this view to inspect their parameters and what their current engines support.

For physical voices, choose Browse installed voices in Home, then press RET or v on an engine to browse its voices. RET on a voice shows details; P plays a named sample. Filters set with F and each view’s selected row and column survive navigation through Home. The engine selected for browsing is a temporary limit: switching to the logical, styles, or engine view removes it. Pressing v from a voice list also removes that temporary limit. Your explicit filters still apply; C clears all filters to show voices across engines. Press q in an engine’s voice list to return to that engine’s row and column. Press q again from the engine list to leave the workbench. The direct view shortcuts remain available.

The engine list reports Availability before routing preferences. An engine with a missing runtime stays visible as unavailable, with its reason under Last failure. Routing policy: allowed means the policy permits using that engine; it does not mean its runtime is installed. Disabled policies, health, and temporary failures are reported separately.

A plays available matching voices in displayed order, skipping unavailable rows. B starts with the selected voice as A and offers another matching voice as B. Choose Search all engines inside that prompt to compare outside the current filters. Both samples use the same sentence. T edits it, F filters, C clears filters, and R refreshes inventory.

Labels identify each engine and voice. Customize emacsvox-aural-preview-label-verbosity for names, brief letters or numbers, or no labels. Labels and samples share one preview sequence: S or foreground navigation cancels the remaining samples. An exact-preview failure is reported instead of accepting a different voice as the requested one. Availability and capability reports describe what the adapter advertises; they are not a listening test of every parameter.
