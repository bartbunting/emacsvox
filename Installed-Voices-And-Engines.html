6.8 Browse Installed Voices And Choose Engines ¶

Use Voice Workbench when the question involves installed voices or engines. Open it from Aural Home with W. Its main views are:

e

Engines, such as Flite or Eloquence. Start here to try voices from a particular synthesizer or inspect its availability.

v

Physical voices: engine voices and, for Omnivox, installed downloads. Use this list to listen, compare, and experiment with their parameters. On an engine row, this key opens just that engine’s voices.

l

Named voices (the Logical voices view): roles such as bolden and annotate. Start here to change which physical voice reads a role.

s

Styles and effects for those named voices. Use this view to inspect their parameters and what their current engines support.

For physical voices, choose Browse voices in Home, then press v on an engine to browse its voices. On an Omnivox engine, RET opens its status, downloads, and settings; q returns to the selected engine. Other speech adapters use RET to browse voices directly. RET or P on a voice plays a named sample; x shows details and t opens the voice editor. Filters set with F and each view’s selected row and column survive navigation through Home. The engine selected for browsing is a temporary limit: switching to the logical, styles, or engine view removes it. Pressing v from a voice list also removes that temporary limit. Your explicit filters still apply; C clears all filters to show voices across engines. Press q in a dedicated engine voice list to return to its details. Press q there to return to the selected engine row and column, and again from the engine list to leave the workbench. The direct view shortcuts remain available.

Languages containing just one voice show that voice directly. Languages with several voices appear under expandable headings. Each heading reports its voice count and whether it is expanded or collapsed; press RET to toggle it. Languages and voices start in alphabetical order, ignoring capitalization. Related regions form a tree: expand English, then United Kingdom to browse voices such as English (West Midlands). Redundant single-child levels are omitted, and private language-tag codes do not become extra headings. Unrecognized language codes remain visible, and voices without a language are sorted under Unknown language. A list containing one language starts expanded. Expansion choices are remembered for each engine scope. Refreshes preserve the selected voice and column, including when new library information arrives. Column sorting keeps headings together with their voices. A samples usable visible voices, including single-voice languages, in displayed order. Expand a heading to include its hidden voices.

Press / to find a voice by name using completion. Search includes collapsed languages and disabled downloads within the current engine and explicit filters. Choosing a voice expands its language and moves to it. If a quick filter hides it, search switches to All voices. Search does not play a sample or change enablement.

Omnivox offers four quick filters. Press Q to choose from their voice counts, or use these shortcuts:

1

All voices: reported engine voices and installed library voices.

2

Downloaded: catalogue downloads, including disabled voices. Imported files and built-in voices remain in All voices.

3

Enabled: library voices selected for the next Apply.

4

Needs Apply: library voices whose desired selection differs from current worker eligibility on either stream. Both enabling and disabling can require Apply. Unchecked voices are counted separately; missing or outdated worker information cannot establish a pending change.

Counts include collapsed languages and respect the current engine and explicit filters. Each quick filter remembers its row and column. After Apply clears Needs Apply, press 2 to return to the downloaded voice for previewing or palette editing. Engine overrides can prevent confirmation; Active and engine details still report usability and runtime health. C also clears the quick filter.

Omnivox’s physical list combines browsing and installed-voice management. Installed shows library membership or an engine-supplied voice; Enabled is the desired selection for the next Apply. Active shows current usability on main speech, notification speech, or both. It does not describe model memory residency. Missing or inconsistent worker evidence appears as Unknown. Library information loads in the background. Press + to enable or disable a managed voice, a to review Apply, and d for downloads. Downloads start disabled; enable and Apply before previewing them. Engine-managed voices retain their engine’s own selection mechanism. Other adapters retain the existing physical inventory view.

To remove a downloaded Piper, Flite or MBROLA package, disable its voices with + and use a to Apply. Then select one of its voices and press C-c u. The review lists every speaker sharing the package, its size, known palette references and any reason files must be retained. Confirming removes only the reviewed managed files. Built-in voices, imported files and engine runtimes stay installed; saved palette choices remain available if you download the voice again.

Another speech session may still need the package. Uninstall retains those files until retirement is confirmed; older session records may require inspection. The result distinguishes removed bytes, remaining files and any unconfirmed deletion amount. After interrupted cleanup, press C-c U or run omnivox-library-resume-uninstall to review and resume the retained operation. This needs an Omnivox build supporting voice uninstallation.

The engine list reports Availability before routing preferences. An engine with a missing runtime stays visible as unavailable, with its reason under Last failure. Routing policy: allowed means the policy permits using that engine; it does not mean its runtime is installed. Disabled policies, health, and temporary failures are reported separately.

A plays available matching voices in displayed order, skipping unavailable rows. B starts with the selected voice as A and offers another matching voice as B. Choose Search all engines inside that prompt to compare outside the current filters. Both samples use the same sentence. T edits it, F filters, C clears filters, and R refreshes inventory.

Labels identify each engine and voice. Customize emacsvox-aural-preview-label-verbosity for names, brief letters or numbers, or no labels. Labels and samples share one preview sequence: S or foreground navigation cancels the remaining samples. An exact-preview failure is reported instead of accepting a different voice as the requested one. Availability and capability reports describe what the adapter advertises; they are not a listening test of every parameter.
